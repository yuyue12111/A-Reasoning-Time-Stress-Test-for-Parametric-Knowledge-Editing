% ====================== ABSTRACT (3 variants; capability-emergent head) ======================
% Choose ONE at submission; trim selected to exactly ~150 words.

% ---- VARIANT A — phenomenon-forward (~152 words) ----
\textbf{Abstract (A).}
Parametric knowledge editing (ROME/MEMIT) is typically scored at \emph{zero} thinking. We show that on native R1-style \emph{reasoning} models, edits are systematically eroded as the chain-of-thought unfolds, and---our central finding---this erosion \emph{emerges with capability}: across two model families and six scales---R1-Distill-Qwen-\{1.5,7,14,32\}B and R1-Distill-Llama-\{8,70\}B on CounterFact---the thinking-induced drop in edit success (ES) is non-significant at small scale but reaches $-10.6$\,pp (significant) at Qwen-32B, while old-answer chain re-emergence and reversion rise monotonically with scale in \emph{both} families (chain-leak rate $0.21\!\to\!0.55$ on Qwen, $0.34\!\to\!0.53$ on Llama). Crucially, a general-purpose Llama distill and a math-specialized Qwen distill erode by the same magnitude at matched scale (Llama-8B$\approx$Qwen-7B, Llama-70B$\approx$Qwen-32B), indicating the driver is capability/scale, not math specialization. A logit-lens analysis indicates reversion is \emph{not} erasure---the edit stays intact at the cloze position (100\% in reverted cases) while the old fact remains encoded mid-network; the chain re-derives it. We then give a training-free, chain-level fix that halves answer reversion ($0.193\!\to\!0.085$) without hurting locality. We report on native R1 only and adopt a word-boundary leakage metric; the six-point progression is a monotone \emph{trend}, not a smooth scaling law---two of the six ES drops are significant (Qwen-32B and the matched Llama-70B, near-identical at $-10.7$\,pp $\approx$ $-10.6$\,pp), and the cross-family match is a consistency statement, not a causal proof.

% ---- VARIANT B — mechanism-and-fix-forward (~150 words) ----
\textbf{Abstract (B).}
We report a \emph{capability-emergent} failure of knowledge editing: parametric edits (ROME) that succeed under zero thinking are increasingly undone as a reasoning model thinks longer, and the effect grows with model scale. On native R1-Distill-Qwen-\{1.5,7,14,32\}B and R1-Distill-Llama-\{8,70\}B / CounterFact, the thinking-induced edit-success drop is non-significant at small scale but significant at the matched high-end point of \emph{both} families ($-10.6$\,pp at Qwen-32B, a near-identical $-10.7$\,pp at Llama-70B); across both families chains re-surface the old fact in a rising fraction of cases (Qwen CLR $20.9\%\!\to\!54.5\%$; Llama $34.0\%\!\to\!52.9\%$) and reversion climbs with scale (Qwen RR $6.1\%\!\to\!19.3\%$; Llama $14.4\%\!\to\!17.5\%$). The general-purpose Llama and math-specialized Qwen distills erode alike at matched scale, isolating capability rather than math specialization as the driver. Mechanistically (logit-lens), the edit is never erased---it stays installed at the cloze position while the suppressed fact persists in mid-layers---so longer reasoning \emph{re-derives} the old answer rather than overwriting the edit. Exploiting this, a training-free intervention that suppresses the edited fact \emph{only within the chain} (not the answer) cuts reversion $0.193\!\to\!0.085$ and removes the thinking tax, while preserving locality ($0.958\!\to\!0.964$); since touching only the chain fixes the answer, it causally confirms chain-internal re-derivation.

% ---- VARIANT C — eval-caution + capability (~151 words) ----
\textbf{Abstract (C).}
Knowledge edits are usually validated at zero thinking, but reasoning models think before answering. We find that test-time reasoning systematically erodes parametric edits, and that this erosion is \emph{capability-emergent}: on R1-Distill-Qwen-\{1.5,7,14,32\}B and R1-Distill-Llama-\{8,70\}B / CounterFact (ROME, word-boundary scoring), the thinking-induced edit-success drop rises from non-significant at small scale to significant at the matched high-end point of \emph{both} families (a near-identical $-10.6$\,pp at Qwen-32B and $-10.7$\,pp at Llama-70B), while old-answer chain re-emergence and reversion climb monotonically with scale in \emph{both} families (Qwen CLR to $\sim$55\%, RR to $\sim$19\%). A general-purpose Llama distill erodes as much as a math-specialized Qwen distill at matched scale, pointing to capability rather than math specialization. A logit-lens study shows the edit remains intact at the cloze position even when the answer reverts---the old fact is re-derived in the chain, not erased from weights. A training-free, chain-only suppression then halves reversion and neutralizes the thinking tax without harming locality, and---by intervening only on the chain---causally pins reversion to chain-internal re-derivation. We also show substring/alias leakage scoring inflates such metrics $\sim$2$\times$. Findings are on native R1 models; the six-point cross-family progression is a monotone trend, not a smooth scaling law (two of the six ES drops are significant---Qwen-32B and the matched Llama-70B, near-identical in magnitude), and the cross-family agreement is a consistency claim, not a causal proof.


% ============================== §1 INTRODUCTION ==============================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Parametric knowledge editing---ROME, MEMIT, AlphaEdit and their descendants---promises to insert, update, or correct a fact directly in a model's weights without retraining. Its success is almost universally measured under a \emph{zero-thinking} protocol: the edited fact is queried directly, and edit success (ES) is read off the immediate next-token prediction or a short greedy continuation. Under this protocol, a single ROME edit on \mbox{R1-Distill-Qwen-32B} succeeds on $59.5\%$ of CounterFact cases.\footnote{Generative-scoring ES with word-boundary matching, $n{=}200$; see \S\ref{sec:setup}. We deliberately separate this from logit-side \texttt{rewrite\_acc}, which is not comparable.} But the models that most need reliable editing are increasingly \emph{reasoning} models that, by default, emit a long chain-of-thought (CoT) before answering. The zero-thinking number says nothing about what the edit is worth once the model is allowed to think.

\paragraph{Thinking erodes edits, and the erosion is capability-emergent.}
When we let the same edited model reason---scaling a natural CoT budget from $B_0$ (no thinking) to $B_3$---edits are systematically pushed back toward the pre-edit answer. On 32B, ES falls from $59.5\%$ at $B_0$ to $49.5\%$ at $B_3$, a drop of $10.6$pp (95\% CI $[3.0, 18.2]$, significant); the original answer re-surfaces somewhere in the reasoning chain in $54.5\%$ of cases (chain leak rate, CLR), and in $19.3\%$ of cases the chain ultimately overturns a zero-thinking success back to the old answer (reversion rate, RR). Crucially, this is not a fixed property of editing: it \emph{emerges with model capability}. On 1.5B the drop is $-2.6$pp and on 7B $-3.0$pp (CI $[-11.0, 5.0]$, not significant---thinking is, if anything, mildly helpful), on 14B it is $4.0$pp (CI $[-3.0, 11.0]$, n.s.), and it becomes both large and significant at the high end of \emph{each} family---$10.6$pp at Qwen-32B and a near-identical $10.7$pp at Llama-70B (CI $[3.2, 18.2]$, significant), the two matched high-end points. The two leakage measures rise monotonically across all four Qwen sizes (CLR $0.209 \to 0.335 \to 0.475 \to 0.545$; RR $0.061 \to 0.080 \to 0.162 \to 0.193$), and---our key cross-family check---the \emph{same} monotone rise appears in the general-purpose R1-Distill-Llama family (8B$\to$70B: CLR $0.340 \to 0.529$, RR $0.144 \to 0.175$, ES drop $0.015 \to 0.107$). At matched scale the two families track each other closely (Llama-8B$\approx$Qwen-7B at CLR $0.34$ vs.\ $0.335$; Llama-70B$\approx$Qwen-32B at CLR $0.529$ vs.\ $0.545$, ES drop $0.107$ vs.\ $0.106$), so erosion follows capability/scale rather than the Qwen-Math specialization. Notably, ES@$B_0$ is \emph{non-decreasing-to-rising} with capability while ES@$B_3$ \emph{falls} at the top end---stronger models install edits at least as well at zero thinking yet pay a larger ``thinking tax,'' so the gap widens precisely as the model gets better. A zero-thinking evaluation does not merely miss this effect; it reports the trend backwards.

\paragraph{The mechanism: reasoning bypasses an edit that is still intact.}
Why does thinking undo an edit? A natural hypothesis is that long generation erases or overwrites the edited weights. Logit-lens analysis on 32B rules this out. On reverted cases, the edit is still fully installed at the editing site: probed at the cloze position, the model predicts the new value in $100\%$ of reverted cases, and the edited fact wins at the top layer. Yet the \emph{old} fact remains encoded in the network's mid-stack: the per-layer new-minus-old gap turns negative around layers 8--12 (the edit layer is 12) before recovering at the top. Reversion is therefore not erasure. The edit sits intact in the weights; the reasoning chain simply routes around it---re-deriving the old fact from collateral, unedited knowledge mid-stream---and the final answer follows the chain rather than the edited site.

\paragraph{A chain-only causal fix.}
This mechanism is directly actionable and, in turn, lets us test it causally. If reversion is driven by the chain re-deriving the old fact, then suppressing the old-answer tokens \emph{within the chain alone}---never touching the answer span---should both reduce reversion and serve as a causal probe. It does. A training-free intervention that suppresses the edited fact's old-value tokens in the think segment ($\text{scope}{=}\texttt{think}$, $\alpha{=}8$) on 32B cuts RR from $0.193$ to $0.085$, raises ES@$B_3$ from $0.495$ to $0.631$, turns the thinking-induced ES drop from $+0.106$ to $-0.035$ (i.e.\ removes the thinking tax), and leaves locality essentially unchanged (Loc $0.958 \to 0.964$). Because we intervene only on the chain yet the \emph{answer} improves, this is causal evidence that in-chain re-derivation, not weight erasure, drives the reversion.\footnote{We are careful about circularity: $\text{scope}{=}\texttt{think}$ directly suppresses the CoT tokens that CLR counts, so we do not treat $\Delta$CLR as primary evidence; our load-bearing effects are $\Delta$RR and $\Delta$ES, both measured on the untouched answer span. See \S\ref{sec:rq3}.}

\paragraph{Honest positioning.}
We do not claim to be the first to observe any of the three ingredients in isolation. That parametric edits fail under realistic, non-teacher-forced inference---and that teacher-forced evaluation overstates editing---has been shown by \citet{he2025benchmarking} (SCR/ReCoE) and, for multi-step reasoning, by \citet{baser2025thinkeval} (ThinkEval). That edited knowledge is ``superficial'' and the original fact remains encoded rather than erased was demonstrated by \citet{xie2025superficial} (Superficial Editing). And training-free decoding-time correction of stale answers was given by \citet{sun2024disco} (DISCO). Our contribution is not the existence of these phenomena but a specific, previously unreported synthesis: that edit erosion is \emph{capability-emergent}; that the mechanism manifests \emph{dynamically inside a native R1 reasoning chain} (intact at the cloze, bypassed in the chain) rather than under crafted static prompts; that a \emph{chain-only} intervention both fixes the answer and causally isolates the cause (versus answer-level decoding contrast in DISCO); and that the CoT-leakage measurement itself must use word-boundary matching.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Capability-emergent erosion (headliner).} We show that test-time reasoning erodes knowledge edits, and that this erosion \emph{emerges with model capability across two model families}: over six scales---R1-Distill-Qwen \{1.5B, 7B, 14B, 32B\} and R1-Distill-Llama \{8B, 70B\}---CLR and RR rise monotonically with scale in both families, the thinking-induced ES drop goes from non-significant at small scale to significant at the matched high-end point of \emph{both} families ($+0.106$ at Qwen-32B and a near-identical $+0.107$ at Llama-70B), and a general-purpose Llama distill erodes as much as the math-specialized Qwen distill at matched scale (Llama-8B$\approx$Qwen-7B, Llama-70B$\approx$Qwen-32B), showing the driver is capability/scale, not math specialization. Zero-thinking evaluation entirely misses---and inverts---this trend. \emph{(We report a monotone six-point trend, not a smooth scaling law: the RR confidence intervals overlap pairwise, two of the six ES drops are individually significant---Qwen-32B ($0.106$) and Llama-70B ($0.107$), the matched high-end point in each family, whose magnitudes are near-identical---while the other four contain zero, and the cross-family match is a consistency statement, not a causal proof.)}
  \item \textbf{Chain-only causal mechanism + fix.} A training-free in-chain suppression of old-fact tokens removes the thinking tax (ES drop $0.106 \to -0.035$) and halves reversion (RR $0.193 \to 0.085$) without harming locality. Because only the chain is touched while the answer improves, this is a \emph{causal} test that in-chain re-derivation---not weight erasure---drives reversion, distinguishing it from answer-level corrections.
  \item \textbf{Dynamic non-erasure inside native CoT.} Via logit-lens we show the edit is intact at the cloze position ($100\%$ installed on reverted cases) while the old fact stays encoded mid-stack (negative new$-$old gap at layers 8--12); reasoning routes around an intact edit, and the answer follows the chain.
  \item \textbf{A measurement caution.} Substring/alias matching inflates CoT-leakage estimates by roughly $2\times$; we adopt and recommend a word-boundary criterion for generative editing evaluation.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent Figure~\ref{fig:capability} previews the headline result: across six model sizes spanning two families, the thinking-induced ES drop, CLR, and RR all trend upward---editing gets more fragile, not more robust, as models reason better---and the two families overlap at matched scale, indicating capability rather than math specialization drives the erosion.


% ============================== §2 RELATED WORK ==============================
\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Our work sits at the intersection of \emph{parametric knowledge editing}, \emph{test-time reasoning}, and \emph{evaluation methodology}. We organize prior art into four threads and, for each, state explicitly what is already established and what remains our contribution. The space is crowded; we therefore make our boundaries precise rather than claim priority.

\paragraph{Editing fails under realistic reasoning (phenomenon).}
A growing body of work shows that locate-then-edit methods (ROME~\citep{meng2022rome}, MEMIT~\citep{meng2023memit}, AlphaEdit~\citep{fang2024alphaedit}), while accurate under teacher-forced cloze probes, degrade once the model is allowed to reason autoregressively or hop across facts. \citet{he2025benchmarking} (SCR/ReCoE) demonstrate that under realistic, non-teacher-forced generation parametric edits collapse and that in-context retrieval outperforms them, concluding that teacher-forcing systematically \emph{over-states} editing success. \citet{baser2025thinkeval} (ThinkEval) construct thought-based knowledge graphs to quantify indirect leakage of stale facts across multi-step reasoning. Multi-hop benchmarks (MQuAKE~\citep{zhong2023mquake}) similarly expose edits that propagate poorly through reasoning chains. We adopt this thread's central finding---that real reasoning erodes edits and that cloze metrics overstate retention---as our \emph{premise}, not our contribution, and we cite it as such.
\textit{Our boundary.} Unlike SCR/ReCoE (which abandons parametric editing for retrieval) and ThinkEval (which uses an externally constructed KG rather than a native reasoning model), we study \emph{native R1-distilled reasoning models} along a continuous \emph{thinking-budget} axis ($B_0$--$B_4$), and we report a property none of these works isolate: erosion is \emph{capability-emergent and cross-family}. Across two distillation lineages and six scales (R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B--32B, R1-Distill-Llama 8B--70B), CLR and RR rise monotonically with scale \emph{within each family} (Qwen CLR $0.209\!\to\!0.545$, Llama $0.340\!\to\!0.529$), the ES drop is significant at two of the six points---the matched high-end point of each family, Qwen-32B ($0.106$, CI $[0.030, 0.182]$) and Llama-70B ($0.107$, CI $[0.032, 0.182]$), near-identical in magnitude---and a general-purpose Llama distill erodes by the same magnitude as a math-specialized Qwen distill at matched scale (Llama-8B$\approx$Qwen-7B, Llama-70B$\approx$Qwen-32B). We present this six-point progression as a \emph{monotone cross-family trend}, not a smooth scaling law---the remaining four per-point ES-drop CIs overlap and contain zero, and the cross-family agreement is a consistency statement, not a causal proof (Section~\ref{sec:rq1}).

\paragraph{Edits are masked, not erased (mechanism).}
Recent mechanistic work argues that editing is \emph{superficial}: the original knowledge is not overwritten but remains encoded elsewhere in the network. \citet{xie2025superficial} (Superficial Editing) show via residual-stream and attention-head / left-singular-vector analysis that standard edited models still encode the pre-edit fact, exposable through crafted prompts. A 2026 cluster on non-erasure reinforces this view, while KELE~\citep{xu2024kele} conversely argues residual knowledge should be actively erased.
\textit{Our boundary.} We do not claim to be first to observe non-erasure---Superficial Editing establishes the core ``encoded-elsewhere'' phenomenon. Our contribution is to show \emph{how} it surfaces in a native reasoning model: via logit-lens on 32B, the edit is \emph{fully intact at the cloze position} in reversion cases (100\% still predict the new value at the top layer), yet a layer-wise gap between new and old logits turns \emph{negative} in the network's mid-stack (layers 8--12, around edit layer 12), indicating the displaced fact remains mid-layer encoded. The reversion is therefore not the chain \emph{erasing} the edit weights but the reasoning chain re-deriving the old fact in-chain and the final answer following the chain---a dynamic, CoT-internal manifestation of non-erasure that crafted static prompts do not capture, and which we causally confirm in Section~\ref{sec:rq3}.

\paragraph{Training-free correction of stale answers (fix).}
Decoding-time and activation-level interventions can steer models away from outdated knowledge without retraining. DISCO~\citep{sun2024disco} (Outdated-Issue-Aware Decoding) contrasts edited- and original-model distributions and amplifies the edited token, cutting outdated zsRE answers to $5.78\%$. Thinking-Intervention and SALT operate within the think segment of reasoning models, and ITI~\citep{li2023iti}, DoLa~\citep{chuang2024dola}, and ParamMute suppress parametric knowledge at inference.
\textit{Our boundary.} DISCO and related decoders intervene at the \emph{answer/output} level (global logit contrast); we instead suppress the edited-fact tokens \emph{only inside the reasoning chain}. This serves a dual purpose. As a fix it roughly halves answer reversion (RR $0.193\!\to\!0.085$), restores edit success at full budget (ES@$B_3$ $0.495\!\to\!0.631$), removes the thinking tax (ES drop $0.106\!\to\!-0.035$), and does not hurt locality (Loc $0.958\!\to\!0.964$). As a causal probe, intervening on the chain alone yet improving the \emph{answer} establishes that in-chain re-derivation drives reversion---a chain-only causal claim DISCO's answer-level design cannot make. We use the edited value itself for suppression, which is legitimate in the editing setting (it is the same target ROME uses to compute the update) and applies only to edited queries. We are careful that the CLR change is partly tautological---$\text{scope}{=}\texttt{think}$ suppresses exactly the chain content we score for old-fact leakage---so our primary evidence rests on $\Delta$RR and $\Delta$ES, which are measured on the answer and are not directly suppressed; the residual CLR ($0.263$) reflects a paraphrase bypass [pending: v2 sequence-level ablation].

\paragraph{Leakage metrics and evaluation methodology.}
A parallel methodological line shows that substring/alias-based matching inflates editing and leakage scores: Mirage/QAEdit~\citep{yao2025mirage} and principled-evaluation and edit-locality critiques document spurious matches, though primarily in non-CoT settings.
\textit{Our boundary.} We extend this concern to CoT leakage: a word-boundary scoring criterion (rejecting sub-token and $<$4-character alias matches) is required to avoid roughly doubling apparent leakage in reasoning traces, and we quantify the inflation ($\sim$2$\times$) directly. All numbers in this paper use the de-contaminated word-boundary scorer and are never mixed with EasyEdit's logit-side \texttt{rewrite\_acc}. We further check that this scorer is not a substring artifact with an \emph{independent semantic validation}: a neutral three-judge panel---prompted only to read an answer and decide whether it commits to the \textsc{old} value, the \textsc{new} value, or \textsc{neither}, never told the edit's status---agrees with our strict reversion metric ($\mathrm{RR}_s$: $o_\text{old}$ asserted and $o_\text{new}$ absent) at Cohen's $\kappa{=}0.84$ (agreement $0.93$, precision $0.92$; $n{=}87$ stratified answers). The loose RR---by design an upper bound that also counts ``edit held but old value mentioned in passing''---agrees less ($\kappa{=}0.39$, precision $0.48$), and the judge confirms this over-count is genuine: true reversion lies \emph{between} $\mathrm{RR}_s$ and RR, exactly the bracket we report. (Judges share our model family, a bias caveat; $33$ further answers were dropped to transient API rate limits and, being mostly non-reversions, would only raise agreement.)

\paragraph{Causal premise.}
Our chain-only intervention builds on evidence that reasoning chains causally shape final answers in R1-distilled models~\citep{anon2025fromreasoning} (From Reasoning to Answer), which we take as the causal prerequisite for attributing reversion to in-chain re-derivation.

\paragraph{Summary of distinctions.}
In sum, the phenomenon (SCR/ReCoE, ThinkEval), the masking-not-erasing mechanism (Superficial Editing), the training-free fix (DISCO), and the metric-inflation caution (Mirage) each have published precedents that we credit explicitly. Our distinct contributions are their \emph{conjunction under a new lens}: capability-emergent erosion across model scale, a chain-only causal intervention, a native R1 + thinking-budget axis, and a CoT-aware word-boundary leakage metric.


% ============================== §3 SETUP AND METHODS ==============================
\section{Setup and Methods}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Models and Editing Backbone}
We study \emph{native} R1-style reasoning models that emit an explicit chain-of-thought delimited by atomic \verb|<think>|/\verb|</think>| tokens before answering. Our capability axis spans \emph{two} R1-distilled families and six scales: \textsc{R1-Distill-Qwen} (math-specialized base) at 1.5B/7B/14B/32B and \textsc{R1-Distill-Llama} (general-purpose base) at 8B/70B. Both use the DeepSeek R1 chat format. Two measurement details we surface up front. (i) \emph{BOS.} Both tokenizers set \texttt{add\_bos\_token=False}; an on/off A/B on Qwen-1.5B shows the leading BOS token has no material effect on Qwen ($\Delta$ES and $\Delta$CLR within one case at $n{=}40$, CIs heavily overlapping), so our legacy BOS-free Qwen runs are valid and the cross-family comparison is not BOS-confounded. Llama degenerates without BOS and is therefore run with it. (ii) \emph{Cross-architecture transfer.} ROME hyper-parameters are ported from the Qwen config; on Llama this over-edited (locality collapse) until we lowered $\mathrm{clamp\_norm\_factor}$ to $2$ for Llama-8B (selected by a locality scan to correct the transfer-induced over-edit; Llama-70B retains the default $4$). The erosion metrics do not hinge on this choice: across $\mathrm{clamp}\in\{2,3,4\}$ on Llama-8B, CLR stays $0.31$--$0.33$, so the cross-family trend is robust to the clamp value (locality is what changes, reaching $\mathrm{Loc}{=}0.835$ at clamp $2$, marginally below our $0.85$ floor). The Llama-70B run uses model parallelism (fp32) and is reported on $n{=}187$ after dropping 13 cases whose generations were numerically degenerate, flagged by a content-based, length-invariant detector. Because degenerate generations are themselves edit failures, dropping them could bias the estimate; we therefore (a) verify the same detector drops $\sim$0 cases on the stable Qwen/Llama-8B runs and (b) report the 70B point both with and without the filter (\S\ref{sec:limitations}); at this sample size its ES drop ($0.107$, CI $[0.032,0.182]$) is now individually significant, matching Qwen-32B at the high end. The Qwen-7B distill is specialized from a \emph{math} base (Qwen2.5-Math); a base probe confirms the unedited 7B already recalls the target facts at $0.59$, so its behavior reflects a math-specialization confound rather than a knowledge bottleneck (\S\ref{sec:limitations})---a confound the cross-family comparison is designed to neutralize. We treat the resulting six-point, two-family progression as a \emph{capability contrast}, never a smooth scaling law.

Edits are applied with \textbf{ROME} as our primary editor on \textbf{CounterFact}; MEMIT is used only for early ablation. We deliberately \emph{keep} parametric editing rather than replacing it with retrieval, which lets us ask whether reasoning erodes an edit that is provably installed (\S\ref{sec:rq2}). The edit layer and locality threshold are selected on the \emph{generative} $B_0$ efficacy (not the editor's logit-level \texttt{rewrite\_acc}); on the 7B/Math base this selects an early-middle layer under a $\mathrm{Loc}\!\ge\!0.85$ floor.

\subsection{Single-Edit Protocol}
Each test fact is evaluated under a strict single-edit protocol: (i)~install one edit $(s,r,o_{\mathrm{old}}\!\to\!o_{\mathrm{new}})$; (ii)~generate at \emph{every} thinking budget while the edit is live; (iii)~restore the base weights before the next fact. Editing and restoring one request at a time eliminates cross-edit accumulation and isolates the effect of test-time reasoning on a clean, freshly installed edit.

A subtle but decisive implementation point: with EasyEdit, ROME/MEMIT must be invoked with \texttt{sequential\_edit=True}. Under \texttt{sequential\_edit=False} the library copies the original weights back \emph{before} \texttt{edit()} returns, so all subsequent generation silently runs on the \emph{unedited} base model (we observed byte-identical outputs across nominally different edit layers). With \texttt{sequential\_edit=True} the edit persists through generation and is undone by our own per-fact \texttt{finally:\,restore} hook, preserving the single-edit semantics.

\subsection{Thinking-Budget Axis ($B_0$--$B_4$)}
We expose test-time compute as a five-point budget axis applied at decode time. $B_0$ is \emph{ZeroThink}: an empty, immediately closed thought block (verbatim reproduction of the ZeroThink template of Jiang et al.~2025, as used by R-TOFU). $B_1$/$B_2$/$B_3$ generalize the \emph{LessThink} idea into a token-budget truncation of the natural chain, with caps of $256$/$1024$/$8192$ thinking tokens; for CounterFact-style facts the chain is typically far shorter than $8192$, so $B_3$ acts as an effective ``natural length.'' $B_4$ additionally forces continuation in the spirit of budget-forcing (s1) by suppressing early stops. We generate greedily for the main results; a $0.6\times3$ sampling arm is used for robustness. We report the zero-thinking point ($B_0$) and the natural-length point ($B_3$) as the two anchors of the ``thinking tax.''

\subsection{Generative Scoring (ES / RR / CLR / Loc)}
We score on \emph{generated} text, not teacher-forced logits; this is distinct from the editor's \texttt{rewrite\_acc} and the two are never mixed. For each fact at each budget we measure:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Edit Success (ES)} --- whether the generated answer states the edited value $o_{\mathrm{new}}$.
  \item \textbf{Reversion Rate (RR)} --- the fraction of facts that succeed at $B_0$ but are pushed back to the \emph{old} answer $o_{\mathrm{old}}$ at $B_3$ (an answer-level reversal).
  \item \textbf{Chain Leak Rate (CLR)} --- the fraction of facts whose reasoning chain re-surfaces $o_{\mathrm{old}}$ at least once (a chain-level leak; need not change the final answer).
  \item \textbf{Locality (Loc)} --- preservation of unrelated facts.
\end{itemize}
The \emph{thinking tax} is the ES drop $\mathrm{ES}@B_0 - \mathrm{ES}@B_3$, and is our primary capability-emergent quantity.

\subsection{Word-Boundary Scoring (Methodological Correction)}
\label{sec:wordbound}
A naive substring match for $o_{\mathrm{old}}$ in generated text, combined with an alias list, systematically inflates leakage. Short ISO/language aliases (e.g.\ \texttt{W}=Vienna, \texttt{in}=India, \texttt{it}=Italian, \texttt{es}=Spain) match inside ordinary words and produce spurious hits. We therefore score with \textbf{word-boundary} matching and drop aliases shorter than four characters. Switching from substring to word-boundary scoring lowers the leakage metrics by roughly $2\times$, which we report as a cautionary finding for generative edit evaluation: substring/alias matching can over-state CoT leakage by about a factor of two (\S\ref{sec:rq1}). All numbers in this paper use the decontaminated word-boundary scorer; pre-decontamination values are not reported.

\subsection{Statistics}
Confidence intervals are $95\%$ percentile bootstrap over edited facts (resampling $n$ facts, $10{,}000$ resamples). Unless stated, $n=200$ per model. We flag an effect as significant only when its bootstrap CI excludes zero; in particular the ES drop is significant at the matched high-end point of each family (Qwen-32B $0.106$, CI $[0.030,0.182]$; Llama-70B $0.107$, CI $[0.032,0.182]$) but not at the smaller scales, and the RR CIs overlap pairwise across model sizes --- hence we claim a \emph{monotone cross-family trend}, not a smooth scaling law. We distinguish \emph{confirmatory} from \emph{exploratory} tests. The RQ3 control battery (the $\mathrm{N}/\mathrm{T}/\mathrm{D}/\mathrm{P}$ arms and their signed predictions, \S\ref{sec:rq3}) is confirmatory: its hypotheses and the placebo donor were pre-registered before any $\mathrm{D}/\mathrm{P}$ result was seen. The per-cell ES-drop significances in RQ1 are, by contrast, \emph{exploratory}: we interpret them descriptively through the pooled capability slope (\S\ref{sec:rq1}, six points across two families), not as six independent confirmatory tests, and we therefore do not attach a corrected family-wise error rate to any single cell. We note for completeness that the two cells whose ES-drop CI excludes zero (Qwen-32B and Llama-70B) have lower bounds $\approx0.030$ and $\approx0.032$ and would in all likelihood survive a Holm correction over the six ES-drop tests; we do not lean on this, as the load-bearing capability evidence is the pooled slope, not per-cell significance.

\section{Editing Erosion is Capability-Emergent}
\label{sec:rq1}

\paragraph{Setup recap.}
For each model we apply ROME to a single CounterFact edit, generate under thinking budgets $B_0$ (no chain-of-thought) through $B_3$ with the edit held in place, then restore the base weights (single-edit protocol, \S\ref{sec:setup}). All scores are generation-side and use word-boundary, contamination-free judging (\S\ref{sec:wordbound}; ROME$\times$CounterFact, greedy). We report three quantities: the \emph{ES drop} $\mathrm{ES}@B_0-\mathrm{ES}@B_3$ (the ``thinking tax''), the \emph{chain-leak rate} (CLR, fraction of chains in which the pre-edit answer resurfaces anywhere in the CoT), and the \emph{reversion rate} (RR, fraction of edits that succeed at $B_0$ but are overturned to the old answer in the final answer at $B_3$). To separate capability from any single base lineage, we span \emph{two} R1-distilled families: \textsc{R1-Distill-Qwen} (distilled from a \emph{math}-specialized base) at $\{1.5, 7, 14, 32\}$B, and \textsc{R1-Distill-Llama} (a \emph{general}-purpose base) at $\{8, 70\}$B.

\paragraph{Main finding: the thinking tax grows with capability, in both families.}
Table~\ref{tab:capability} and Figure~\ref{fig:capability} show that as model scale increases, test-time reasoning erodes edits more severely, and this holds \emph{within each family independently}. For Qwen the ES drop moves from $-0.026$ (1.5B, n.s.) and $-0.030$ (7B, n.s.) through $0.040$ (14B, n.s.) to $0.106$ at 32B (95\% CI $[0.030,0.182]$, individually significant); for Llama it moves from $0.015$ (8B, n.s.) to $0.107$ at 70B (95\% CI $[0.032,0.182]$, also individually significant). The two families' high-end points are thus the matched scale anchors and their ES drops are near-identical ($0.106 \approx 0.107$), both significant. The two companion leakage measures rise \emph{monotonically} with scale in both families: Qwen CLR climbs $0.209\!\to\!0.335\!\to\!0.475\!\to\!0.545$ and RR climbs $0.061\!\to\!0.080\!\to\!0.162\!\to\!0.193$; Llama CLR climbs $0.340\!\to\!0.529$ and RR climbs $0.144\!\to\!0.175$. The consistent reading is a single emergent phenomenon: stronger reasoners are \emph{more} prone to re-deriving the suppressed fact inside the chain and following it in the final answer. Notably, ES@$B_0$ is \emph{non-decreasing-to-rising} with capability (Qwen rises at the top end $0.555\!\to\!0.595$, modulo the Math-base 7B dip; Llama is roughly flat, $0.625\!\to\!0.610$) while ES@$B_3$ \emph{falls} at the top end---stronger models install edits at least as well at zero thinking yet pay a larger thinking tax, so the gap widens precisely as the model gets better.

\paragraph{Cross-family alignment: erosion tracks capability, not Math specialization.}
The headline structure of Table~\ref{tab:capability} is not the within-family slope alone but the \emph{agreement across families at matched scale}. A general-purpose Llama and a Math-specialized Qwen of comparable size show erosion of the same order: Llama-8B aligns with Qwen-7B (CLR $0.340$ vs.\ $0.335$; RR $0.144$ vs.\ $0.080$; ES drop $0.015$ vs.\ $-0.030$), and Llama-70B aligns with Qwen-32B (CLR $0.529$ vs.\ $0.545$; RR $0.175$ vs.\ $0.193$; ES drop $0.107$ vs.\ $0.106$). At the matched high-end point the two families' ES drops are now essentially identical ($0.107$ vs.\ $0.106$) and \emph{both individually significant}, a striking cross-family-symmetric result that strengthens the capability-emergent claim beyond a single point. Because the two families differ in base lineage (general vs.\ math) yet land at one magnitude when matched on scale, the natural driver of erosion is \emph{scale/capability}, not a Math-specialization artifact of the Qwen distills. We state this as \emph{consistency/alignment} across families, not a proof of causation; it removes the most obvious confound (that erosion is peculiar to math-tuned models) without licensing a mechanistic causal claim, which we reserve for RQ3.

\paragraph{Decomposing the tax: ES@$B_0$ rises while ES@$B_3$ falls.}
The ES drop grows with scale for two compounding reasons (Figure~\ref{fig:capability}, ES panel). Zero-thinking edit success is flat-to-rising with capability ($\mathrm{ES}@B_0$: Qwen rises at the top end $0.555\!\to\!0.595$, Llama is roughly flat $0.625\!\to\!0.610$)---larger models install edits at least about as reliably at $B_0$. Yet edit success \emph{after} reasoning declines much harder at the top end ($\mathrm{ES}@B_3$: Qwen $0.515\!\to\!0.495$, Llama $0.610\!\to\!0.503$, a $0.107$ fall). The thinking tax is thus the widening scissor between a non-decreasing-to-rising $B_0$ ceiling and a $B_3$ floor that drops sharply at the top: capability does not hurt edit installation at zero thinking but sharply hurts edit survival once the model is allowed to reason, so the $B_0$--$B_3$ gap widens with scale.

\paragraph{Honest scoping of the trend (not a scaling law).}
We do \emph{not} claim a smooth scaling law. The evidence is a six-point monotone \emph{trend} in CLR and RR across two families, not a pairwise-significant progression. Concretely: (i) the ES drop is statistically significant at \emph{two} of the six points---the matched high-end point of each family, Qwen-32B ($0.106$, CI $[0.030,0.182]$) and Llama-70B ($0.107$, CI $[0.032,0.182]$, $n{=}187$), whose magnitudes are near-identical; the other four ES-drop CIs span zero. We emphasize this is two independently significant points, not a single anchor, but still not a pairwise-resolved progression. (ii) The RR confidence intervals overlap pairwise within Qwen ($[0.018,0.105]$, $[0.030,0.140]$, $[0.099,0.234]$, $[0.126,0.269]$) and across the matched cross-family pairs, so we assert direction and consistency, not pairwise separation. (iii) We report the largest RR ($0.193$ at Qwen-32B) as ``$\approx$$0.19$'' and never round it past $0.20$. The clean, load-bearing signal is the monotone rise of CLR (and RR) with scale \emph{in each family}, reinforced by the cross-family alignment; we lead with CLR/RR rather than with the mostly-non-significant ES drop.

\paragraph{Emergence as a pooled slope.} To put the capability dependence on a statistical footing that does not rely on per-cell significance, we pool all six points across both families and regress each erosion metric on $\log_{10}$ parameters. The slope is significantly positive for all three metrics: ES drop $0.095$ per decade (95\% CI $[0.033, 0.158]$, $R^2{=}0.82$), RR $0.081$ ($[0.019, 0.143]$, $R^2{=}0.77$), and CLR $0.218$ ($[0.121, 0.315]$, $R^2{=}0.91$), with a point-resampling bootstrap $p(\text{slope}\le 0) < 0.002$ in every case. The slope is positive \emph{within} each family as well, and for the ES drop it is near-identical across families (Qwen $0.098$, Llama $0.098$ per decade). We read this as a trend-level statistical claim---a pooled positive slope of erosion against a capability proxy ($\log$-parameters, justified by the cross-family alignment)---\emph{not} a fitted scaling law: with six points it establishes the \emph{direction and significance} of the dependence, not its functional form (\texttt{src/emergence\_regression.py}). These slopes survive influence deletion: every leave-one-point-out refit keeps all three slopes positive, the CLR slope stays significant under \emph{every} single-point deletion, and---most stringently---the CLR slope remains significant within the Qwen family alone (dropping the entire Llama family: $0.22$, 95\% CI $[0.12, 0.40]$). The ES-drop and RR slopes are direction-robust but their significance leans on the cross-family pooling (their within-Qwen CIs contain zero), which is the honest reason we foreground CLR.

\paragraph{Caveats on the cross-family endpoints.}
Two cross-family points carry measurement caveats that we surface here and re-state in \S\ref{sec:setup}/\S\ref{sec:limitations}. \emph{Llama-8B}: porting Qwen hyper-parameters over-edited Llama, so we set $\mathrm{clamp\_norm\_factor}{=}2$ (selected by a locality scan; Llama-70B keeps the default $4$). This is a per-model locality correction, but it does \emph{not} drive the erosion result: across $\mathrm{clamp}\in\{2,3,4\}$ the CLR stays $0.31$--$0.33$, only locality moves. At clamp $2$, $\mathrm{Loc}{=}0.835$, just \emph{below} our $0.85$ target and reported as marginal. \emph{Llama-70B}: run under model parallelism ($2\times4$, fp32), which produced intermittent numerically-unstable generations; we drop $13$ such cases via a content-based, length-invariant degeneracy detector, yielding $n{=}187$. Because degenerate generations are themselves edit failures, this discard is \emph{not} unbiased; we therefore report the point both with and without the filter. At this sample size the 70B ES drop ($0.107$, CI $[0.032,0.182]$) is now \emph{individually significant}, matching Qwen-32B at the high end---so it is an independently significant result rather than a directional anchor only; the content-triggered discard remains the one caveat on its point estimate.

\paragraph{The 7B confound (within Qwen).}
\textsc{R1-Distill-Qwen-7B} is distilled from Qwen2.5-\emph{Math}-7B, so within the Qwen family the $7\text{B}\!\to\!14\text{B}\!\to\!32$B axis mixes scale with math specialization; the flat-to-negative 7B ES drop is therefore not clean evidence of ``no erosion at small scale.'' Importantly, this is \emph{not} a knowledge bottleneck: a base-knowledge probe shows the unedited 7B already recalls the target facts at $0.59$, so its low post-edit ES reflects edit/reasoning interaction, not ignorance. The cross-family comparison above is precisely what neutralizes this confound at the family level: Llama (general) reproduces Qwen's (math) erosion magnitude at matched scale, so the trend is not an artifact of the Qwen math lineage.

\paragraph{Beyond single-hop cloze: erosion replicates on multi-hop propagation.}
A reviewer might worry the erosion is an artifact of single-hop cloze scoring. We test this with a multi-hop propagation probe on \textsc{R1-Distill-Llama-8B} (MQuAKE-CF): we apply ROME to a single fact, then ask a \emph{two-hop} question whose surface never names $o_\text{old}$, and measure whether the edit propagates to the downstream answer and whether thinking erodes that propagation. To make the test informative we first gate to a clean residual pool ($n{=}150$)---dropping cases the unedited base already answers (prior-dominated) and one degenerate relation template whose new-gold answer collapses onto a single entity. On the cases where the edit \emph{does} propagate at zero thinking (answers $o_\text{new}$'s downstream consequence and not $o_\text{old}$'s; $n_{B_0}{=}48$), thinking erodes that propagation in $18.8\%$ of cases (95\% CI $[0.083, 0.292]$, paired bootstrap), carried by the most diverse, best-powered template (citizenship$\to$head-of-state, $n{=}57$: $0.148$, $[0.037, 0.296]$). This is the \emph{same within-case signature} as single-hop reversion at this scale (8B RR $0.144$, $[0.088, 0.208]$): a real erosion of installed edits masked at the margin. We report the gated within-case estimand rather than the marginal for a structural reason---a two-hop question requires reasoning tokens to traverse the hop, so zero thinking cannot express the propagation at all (marginal $\mathrm{hop\text{-}ES}$ \emph{rises} $0.32\!\to\!0.51$ as thinking both completes un-propagated chains and erodes propagated ones); the net-positive marginal is an artifact of zero-thinking's inability to multi-hop, and the within-case erosion is the matched analogue of single-hop RR. The erosion is predominantly to \emph{neither} answer ($8$ of $9$ eroded cases) rather than to $o_\text{old}$: at multi-hop the edit's propagation \emph{decays to noise} rather than the old downstream fact resurging. We scope this honestly as a \emph{single-template multi-hop pilot}: a single family at one scale, on templated MQuAKE pairs, with pooled significance carried by one relation template (citizenship$\to$head-of-state, P27); the other two templates are under-powered ($n_{B_0}{\le}8$) and individually contain zero. It therefore establishes that the erosion is \emph{not purely a single-hop cloze artifact}, but is \emph{not} a clean or generalized multi-hop scaling result (a multi-relation, multi-scale multi-hop study---including 32B---is future work).

\paragraph{Takeaway.}
Across six models spanning two distillation lineages, editing gets \emph{more} fragile---not more robust---as models reason better: CLR, RR, and the ES drop all trend upward with scale within each family, the families align at matched scale, and a zero-thinking evaluation misses (and at the top end inverts) this trend. We carry this monotone, cross-family trend---explicitly a trend and not a scaling law, with two matched high-end points (Qwen-32B and Llama-70B) individually significant and near-identical in magnitude---into the mechanism (\S\ref{sec:rq2}) and the chain-only causal fix (\S\ref{sec:rq3}).


% ============================== §5 RQ2 — MECHANISM ==============================
\section{Why Does Reasoning Undo the Edit? (Mechanism)}
\label{sec:rq2}

RQ1 establishes that test-time reasoning erodes edits and that the erosion is capability-emergent. A natural worry is that the long chain-of-thought (CoT) somehow \emph{destroys} the edited parameters---that decoding hundreds of tokens perturbs the model state and the ROME update decays. We show this is \emph{not} what happens. The edit remains fully installed; reasoning instead routes \emph{around} it, re-derives the old fact inside the chain, and the final answer follows the chain. The logit-lens probe below is on the 32B model (the regime where the phenomenon is strongest); the route taxonomy that follows it spans three Qwen scales (7B/14B/32B), over the gated reversions (ROME$\times$CounterFact, greedy).

\paragraph{The edit is intact, not erased (logit-lens at the cloze).}
For every reverted case---cases where the model answers the new (edited) value at zero budget $B_0$ but reverts to the old value after thinking at $B_3$---we re-probe the edited fact at the original cloze position (subject + relation prompt, no CoT) and read the top-of-stack logits. The edit is installed in \textbf{100\%} of reverted cases: the final-layer prediction at the cloze is still $o_{\mathrm{new}}$. The ROME weights are not perturbed by decoding; the reversion is therefore not a parameter-erasure effect. Whatever the chain does, it does so on a network that still ``knows'' the edited value when asked directly. This is the load-bearing observation of RQ2: the failure is a \emph{reasoning} failure, not a \emph{storage} failure.

\paragraph{The old fact is still encoded in the middle of the network.}
If the edit sits intact at the top but the model can still revert, the old fact must remain readable somewhere. We confirm this with a layer-wise logit-lens sweep. We define the per-layer \emph{gap} $g_\ell = \mathrm{logit}_\ell(o_{\mathrm{new}}) - \mathrm{logit}_\ell(o_{\mathrm{old}})$ at the cloze, decoded through the unembedding at each sampled layer $\ell$ (of $64$ total; edit applied at layer $12$). The gap is \emph{negative} in the early-middle band---$g_8 = -0.246$, $g_{12} = -0.351$ (Figure~\ref{fig:rq2})---meaning the old value $o_{\mathrm{old}}$ is favored at layers 8--12, right around the edit layer. The gap then climbs steadily and is strongly positive in the upper stack (peaking at $g_{56} = 17.8$ and remaining large at the final layer, $g_{64} = 11.6$), where the ROME edit dominates and produces the intact top-layer $o_{\mathrm{new}}$. So the network carries both readings simultaneously: a middle-layer trace of the original fact and a late, edit-installed trace of the new fact. The edit overwrites the surface readout but leaves an intact mid-network substrate from which the old fact can be reconstructed---which is exactly the substrate a multi-step chain can latch onto.

\paragraph{Mechanism: route around, re-derive in-chain, answer follows the chain.}
Putting the two together: the edit is installed (intact cloze) yet the old fact is still encoded mid-network, and during a long CoT the model retrieves and re-derives the old value through ordinary reasoning steps---bridging through related facts rather than reading the edited slot---and the final answer tracks the chain rather than the edited parameter. Reversion is thus \emph{reasoning bypassing a perfectly good edit}, not the chain wearing the edit away. We make this causal in RQ3 (\S\ref{sec:rq3}): suppressing the old-fact tokens \emph{only inside the chain} (never at the answer) halves final-answer reversion, which only makes sense if the in-chain re-derivation is what drives the answer back.

\paragraph{Taxonomy of reversion routes.}
The logit-lens probe establishes that in every reverted case the edit remains installed at the cloze (top-of-stack $=o_{\mathrm{new}}$ with no chain-of-thought); reversion is therefore not weight erasure but the native chain \emph{routing around} an intact edit. We characterise these routes with a four-way \emph{descriptive} taxonomy, applied only to a gated true-reversion population (B0 commits $o_{\mathrm{new}}$ and not $o_{\mathrm{old}}$; the B3 \emph{committed} answer commits $o_{\mathrm{old}}$; held edits, degenerate or looping decodes, and non-separable $o_{\mathrm{old}}/o_{\mathrm{new}}$ pairs---short codes and morphological variants---are excluded). \textbf{Recall}: the chain asserts $o_{\mathrm{old}}$ flat as the queried relation value with no distinct intermediate, a stored fact about the entity. \textbf{Bridge} (the modal route): the chain derives $o_{\mathrm{old}}$ through a named intermediate---subject-name etymology, geography, category, brand, a confabulated link, or an edit-respecting forward drift that passes through $o_{\mathrm{new}}$ without contesting it. \textbf{Reflective-override}: the chain verbalises $o_{\mathrm{new}}$ as a candidate, a reflection marker intervenes, and the same span commits to $o_{\mathrm{old}}$; we define this purely by the rhetorical move and make no claim that $o_{\mathrm{old}}$ is correct---the cloze edit is intact at every such case. \textbf{Associative}: a residual catch-all for traceless reversions, split into an \emph{implicit-leak} sub-case (where $o_{\mathrm{old}}$ never appears even as meaning yet the answer follows an unspoken prior over the intact edit) and a non-committal \emph{ramble}. The draft's recall/bridge/dismiss does not survive as a flat three-way: recall and bridge survive one-to-one, dismiss is reframed and tightened into Reflective-override (engagement of $o_{\mathrm{new}}$ + same-span polarity to $o_{\mathrm{old}}$, distinguishing it from the $o_{\mathrm{new}}$-defending chains that dominate the surface-reflection pool and are gated out as held), and a fourth Associative class is mandatory for collective exhaustiveness---it is precisely the bucket word-boundary chain-leakage matching is blind to and the cleanest evidence for the un-verbalised-prior reading. An orthogonal binary \emph{contests-edit} stance (chain verbatim calls $o_{\mathrm{new}}$ wrong) is reported as a second column, cross-cutting all four routes; contesting language is not edit erasure. The taxonomy is descriptive and orthogonal to the logit-lens finding; it documents the \emph{routes} the reasoning takes, all of which leave the cloze edit intact. Three independent judges label each chain from the raw chain-of-thought under a strict precedence (Reflective-override $>$ Bridge $>$ Recall $>$ Associative) with a required verbatim quoted span; per-route counts are reported with by-case bootstrap CIs and inter-judge Fleiss' $\kappa$, and---because the gated true-reversion counts are small---low-count routes are read as qualitative route frequencies and we pool across model scales.

We classify the gated reversions for ROME (greedy) at three Qwen scales ($n{=}4/5/10$ committed reversions at 7B/14B/32B, $19$ pooled, after excluding held edits and short-code/morphological pairs; Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}); the picture is sharply concentrated. \textbf{Bridge is the dominant route} (pooled $17/19$, $90\%$, 95\% CI $[74,100]$): reversions are \emph{reconstructive}---the chain derives $o_{\mathrm{old}}$ through a named intermediate (subject-name etymology, geography, category, or a confabulated link)---not flat memory recall. \textbf{Recall and Associative are empty} at every scale (pooled $0/19$): no chain merely states $o_{\mathrm{old}}$ as a remembered fact, and even the two chains where $o_{\mathrm{old}}$ never appears verbatim are judged Bridge via a quotable spelling/etymology cue, so there is no truly traceless ``implicit-leak'' in the committed set. \textbf{Reflective-override appears only at the largest scale}: it occurs at 32B ($2/10$; both also verbatim contest the edit) and at neither 7B nor 14B, where every committed reversion is a Bridge. This pattern is \emph{consistent with} a capability-dependent route---verbalising the edited value, doubting it, and overriding it plausibly requires the same capability that drives the headline erosion, whereas smaller models reach $o_{\mathrm{old}}$ only by surface-cue bridging---but with a gated true-reversion population of only $4/5/10$ chains ($19$ pooled) it is too small to \emph{establish} a capability-dependent difference, and we offer it as a suggestive route observation rather than a second emergence claim. Inter-judge agreement is moderate where categories vary (Fleiss' $\kappa{=}0.59$ at 32B, $0.52$ pooled; undefined at 7B/14B, which are unanimously Bridge). The high held-exclusion rate (e.g.\ $6/16$ judged 32B chains commit $o_{\mathrm{new}}$ with $o_{\mathrm{old}}$ only conceded) also confirms that the loose RR over-counts reversion relative to this judge-committed measure, consistent with our strict $\mathrm{RR}_s$.

\paragraph{Relation to ``superficial editing.''}
That edits are superficial---installed at the surface while the original knowledge persists deeper in the network---is itself established. \citet{xie2025superficial} (Superficial Editing) show via residual-stream and attention-head analysis that standard edited models still encode the original knowledge, and \citet{he2025benchmarking} (SCR/ReCoE) show parametric edits collapse under realistic autoregressive decoding. We do \textbf{not} claim to be the first to find non-erasure. Our contribution is narrower and specific to reasoning: prior non-erasure evidence is \emph{static}, surfaced with crafted trigger prompts on standard models; ours arises \emph{dynamically inside a native R1 chain-of-thought}, we show the edit is simultaneously intact at the cloze (100\%) while the old fact re-emerges mid-stack, and we tie the reversion to the chain causally (\S\ref{sec:rq3}, chain-only intervention). The mechanism is the bridge between RQ1's capability-emergent erosion and RQ3's chain-level fix.

% ============================== §6 RQ3 — CHAIN-LEVEL TRAINING-FREE FIX ==============================
\section{A Chain-Level, Training-Free Fix}
\label{sec:rq3}

Section~\ref{sec:rq2} located the failure mode: the edit is intact at the cloze site (top-layer prediction is $o_\text{new}$ in 100\% of the reverted cases), and the old fact is never removed from the network --- it remains encoded in the mid layers and is \emph{re-derived inside the reasoning chain}, after which the final answer follows the chain. This diagnosis prescribes the intervention point directly: do not touch the edited weights, do not touch the answer; instead suppress the re-emergence of the old fact \emph{where it re-emerges}, i.e.\ token-by-token inside the thinking chain.

\paragraph{Method.}
Given an edited model and a query whose target has been edited from $o_\text{old}$ to $o_\text{new}$, we tokenize $o_\text{old}$ and, at every decoding step that falls inside the \texttt{<think>}\ldots\texttt{</think>} span ($\textsc{scope}{=}\textsc{think}$), subtract a constant penalty $\alpha$ from the logits of those tokens before sampling. The post-think answer is decoded with the unmodified logits. The intervention is training-free, requires no extra forward passes, and is gated on edited queries only: the value $o_\text{old}$ is already known in any editing scenario (it is the same quantity ROME/MEMIT use to compute the update), so this introduces no information the editor did not already have. We report a single operating point, $\alpha{=}8$, $\textsc{scope}{=}\textsc{think}$, on R1-Distill-Qwen-32B, CounterFact, $n{=}200$, greedy decoding at the $B_3$ budget.

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~\ref{tab:rq3} contrasts the model with suppression off (the $B_3$ baseline of Section~\ref{sec:rq1}) versus on. Chain-level suppression \textbf{halves the final-answer reversion rate} (RR $0.193 \rightarrow 0.085$), \textbf{recovers thinking-budget edit success} (ES@$B_3$ $0.495 \rightarrow 0.631$, turning the $+0.106$ thinking tax into $-0.035$, i.e.\ ES@$B_3$ now slightly \emph{exceeds} ES@$B_0$), and \textbf{does not harm locality} (Loc $0.958 \rightarrow 0.964$). CLR drops from $0.545$ to $0.263$.

\paragraph{Causal interpretation: intervening only on the chain.}
The intervention modifies \emph{only} the thinking-span logits; the post-think answer is decoded normally. That it nonetheless improves the final answer (RR halved, ES@$B_3$ recovered) closes the causal loop from Section~\ref{sec:rq2}: the answer reverts \emph{because} the old fact is re-derived in the chain, not because the edit was erased. This is the chain-only causal handle that distinguishes our diagnosis from a purely observational one.

\paragraph{Causal specificity: a pre-registered control battery.} A skeptic could object that \emph{any} chain perturbation, not specifically suppressing $o_\text{old}$, might help. We rule this out with a four-arm battery on the same 32B/CounterFact cases ($\textsc{scope}{=}\textsc{think}$, $\alpha{=}8$, paired by case; predictions and a frequency/length-matched placebo word pre-registered before seeing results): \textbf{N} (no suppression), \textbf{T} (suppress $o_\text{old}$, our fix), \textbf{D} (suppress $o_\text{new}$, a directional control), and \textbf{P} (suppress a matched but irrelevant placebo token---a frequency- and length-matched \emph{inert} word, not a competing answer). We are precise about what this isolates: $\mathrm{P}$ controls for the act of suppressing \emph{some} inert token of matched surface statistics, so $\mathrm{T}-\mathrm{P}$ establishes that the effect is specific to a \emph{meaningful competing target} ($o_\text{old}$) relative to an inert frequency/length-matched placebo floor; it does not establish specificity against \emph{any} strong competing-answer token (the directional arm $\mathrm{D}$, which suppresses $o_\text{new}$, addresses sign rather than that distinct alternative). The pre-registered signed ladder holds on ES@$B_3$: $\mathrm{D}\,(0.281) < \mathrm{N}\,(0.495) \approx \mathrm{P}\,(0.507) < \mathrm{T}\,(0.631)$. Three between-arm paired-bootstrap contrasts (by case) confirm the effect is signed and rises above the inert-placebo floor. (i) \emph{The placebo is inert}: P$-$N is $\approx 0$ on every answer metric (RR $0.000$, 95\% CI $[-0.057,0.057]$; ES $+0.015$, $p{=}0.71$), so the fix is not a generic chain perturbation. (ii) \emph{The effect is direction-specific}: suppressing $o_\text{new}$ instead (D$-$N) does the \emph{opposite}---ES $-0.217$ $[-0.289,-0.144]$, RR $+0.086$ $[0.019,0.152]$. (iii) \emph{The $o_\text{old}$ effect survives above the placebo floor}: our headline contrast T$-$P excludes zero on all metrics---RR $-0.095$ $[-0.171,-0.029]$, CLR $-0.228$ $[-0.294,-0.162]$, ES $+0.122$ $[0.051,0.193]$, RR$_s$ $-0.057$ $[-0.114,-0.010]$. We therefore report the $o_\text{old}$ effect \emph{above placebo} (T$-$P), not against zero. With the $\alpha$ sweep providing the dose axis, the fix is thus established as a \emph{signed, specific, dose-controlled} causal manipulation rather than an artifact of disturbing the chain. (The one residual: the placebo marginally lowers CLR by $0.05$ ($p{=}0.054$)---a small chain-perturbation effect on in-chain leakage---but it is dwarfed by $o_\text{old}$'s $-0.276$, and the $o_\text{old}$-above-placebo CLR margin remains $-0.228$, $p<0.001$.)

\paragraph{A deeper non-independence between RQ2 and RQ3 (named, bounded, not yet closed).} Beyond the narrow metric-circularity of $\Delta$CLR (below), a sharper objection cuts across the two results: RQ2's load-bearing observation (the edit is intact at the cloze in $100\%$ of reverted cases) and RQ3's fix (suppressing $o_\text{old}$ \emph{in the chain} repairs the answer) may not be independent --- both ultimately read off the \emph{same} underlying logit geometry around the edited slot, so RQ3 risks ``confirming'' RQ2 by re-measuring the very quantity RQ2 asserts rather than by exercising an independent causal pathway. We take this seriously and bound it rather than claim it away. Two facts make the support more than a single geometry read twice. First, RQ3's load-bearing effects --- $\Delta$RR and $\Delta$ES@$B_3$ --- are scored on the \emph{answer} span, which $\textsc{scope}{=}\textsc{think}$ never touches (the post-think answer is decoded with unmodified logits); an improvement there is a behavioral consequence of altering the chain, not a re-readout of the cloze logits that RQ2 inspects. Second, the pre-registered N/T/D/P battery shows the repair is \emph{signed} and \emph{$o_\text{old}$-specific}: an inert placebo (P$-$N $\approx 0$) and a reversed-sign directional control (D$-$N, the opposite effect) rule out the reading on which a pure circularity would rest --- that any perturbation coupled to the same logit structure would move the answer the same way. So the fix is not merely re-reading the cloze geometry. What these checks do \emph{not} do is fully sever the dependence: both RQ2's probe and RQ3's intervention still act on a network whose $o_\text{old}$/$o_\text{new}$ logit structure is shared, and neither manipulates the in-chain re-derivation while holding that structure fixed. Cleanly breaking the circularity requires a chain-substitution mediation experiment (E-CHAINSUB) that decodes the same answer under matched, counterfactual chains --- a native reversion chain ($\mathrm{T0}$), a chain that states the edited fact without $o_\text{old}$ or a bridge ($\mathrm{T\text{-}clean}$), and a chain that re-derives $o_\text{old}$ through a named bridge ($\mathrm{T\text{-}old}$) --- and tests whether $\mathrm{T\text{-}clean}$ pulls the answer back to $o_\text{new}$ (necessity) and $\mathrm{T\text{-}old}$ reverts it even with the edit fully in place (sufficiency). That experiment is reserved for the camera-ready version (a small $n{\approx}17$--$30$ 32B case study); here we name the worry and bound it, and do \emph{not} claim it is resolved.

\paragraph{On the partial circularity of $\Delta$CLR.}
We deliberately do \emph{not} lead with the CLR reduction. CLR measures whether $o_\text{old}$ surfaces \emph{in the chain}, and $\textsc{scope}{=}\textsc{think}$ suppresses exactly those chain tokens, so part of $\Delta$CLR is mechanical (the metric and the intervention act on the same span). Our load-bearing evidence is therefore $\Delta$RR and $\Delta$ES, which are measured on the \emph{answer} --- a span the intervention never touches --- and on which the improvement is genuinely earned. The residual CLR of $0.263$ (rather than $\approx 0$) is itself informative: it reflects a paraphrase bypass (the old fact re-stated via aliases the penalty does not cover), which we probe with a sequence-level variant in the ablations \textbf{[pending]}.

\paragraph{Relation to DISCO.}
The closest training-free decoding fix is DISCO~\citep{sun2024disco}, which contrasts the edited and original models' \emph{output} distributions to amplify the edited token. Our intervention differs in locus and granularity: it operates \emph{inside the reasoning chain at the token level} ($\textsc{scope}{=}\textsc{think}$), not on the answer-level distribution, and it is the act of intervening on the chain alone --- while the answer follows --- that doubles as the causal probe. We do not claim a stronger remediation than DISCO; we claim a different, chain-localized intervention that is coupled to, and validates, the re-derivation mechanism.

\paragraph{Collateral damage on non-edit traffic.}
The fix is reported at one operating point on one model (32B), one editor (ROME) and one dataset (CounterFact). By construction the suppressor fires \emph{only} on queries about edited facts; on every other input the model runs with unmodified logits, so general-capability traffic such as GSM8K or MATH is untouched \emph{by design}. The relevant safety question is therefore not the average collateral cost but the worst case under mis-application. To bound that, we run a deliberately \emph{pathological} stress test (\texttt{src/genbench.py}): we suppress the \emph{union} of $o_\text{old}$ first-tokens over \emph{all} edited facts with $\textsc{scope}{=}\textsc{all}$ (penalising the answer span as well as the chain) on un-edited GSM8K/MATH items. This compounds three departures from deployment at once---all facts simultaneously, every span, every query---and is thus a strict \emph{upper} bound, not the operating regime. Under this bound we observe $\Delta\text{acc}{=}-0.15$ on GSM8K ($0.535\!\to\!0.385$, $n{=}200$) and $+0.04$ on MATH ($0.150\!\to\!0.190$, $n{=}100$); the GSM8K figure exceeds our $|\Delta\text{acc}|\!\le\!0.02$ gate, and we report this candidly rather than claiming the gate is passed. Two caveats bound its interpretation. First, no deployment instantiates this union-with-\textsc{all}-scope construction---the deployed intervention is single-fact, $\textsc{scope}{=}\textsc{think}$, and gated to edited queries, under which non-edit accuracy is unchanged by construction. Second, the \emph{absolute} accuracies are depressed by a harness artifact: our budget controller caps the thinking span at $8192$ tokens with a $256$-token answer window (\texttt{think\_budget.py}), and 32B MATH chains routinely exceed $8192$ and are truncated before $\boxed{\cdot}$, deflating \emph{both} arms; these absolutes should not be read as the model's true GSM8K/MATH ability ($\sim$0.95/$\sim$0.90 elsewhere). We therefore also run the deployment-faithful gate---single-fact, $\textsc{scope}{=}\textsc{think}$, with an answer-reaching budget ($16{,}384$-token think cap, $512$-token answer window)---which is the appropriate measurement. It \emph{passes}: applying the single-fact suppressor to un-edited GSM8K/MATH leaves accuracy essentially unchanged, $\Delta\text{acc}{=}{+}0.005$ on GSM8K ($0.855\!\to\!0.860$, $n{=}200$) and $0.000$ on MATH ($0.700\!\to\!0.700$, $n{=}100$), both within the $|\Delta\text{acc}|\!\le\!0.02$ gate. The recovered absolute accuracies ($0.855$/$0.700$, versus $0.535$/$0.150$ under the truncating worst-case harness) confirm that the depressed worst-case absolutes were the budget-truncation artifact, not the model; and this single-fact-applied-to-\emph{all}-queries test is itself still an upper bound, since the deployed suppressor fires only on edited queries (non-edit traffic is unchanged by construction). \paragraph{Dose-response and the scope ablation.} A penalty sweep ($\alpha\in\{2,4,8,12,16\}$, $\textsc{scope}{=}\textsc{think}$, $n{=}100$) shows a clean dose-response: CLR falls monotonically with $\alpha$ ($0.545\!\to\!0.444\!\to\!0.384\!\to\!0.268\!\to\!0.146\!\to\!0.082$), RR and RR$_s$ are driven toward zero, ES is restored and peaks around $\alpha{\approx}8$--$12$, and---crucially---locality is flat across the \emph{entire} sweep ($\mathrm{Loc}\,{\approx}\,0.958$ at every $\alpha$). Our operating point $\alpha{=}8$ thus sits near the ES peak of a smooth curve rather than at a cherry-picked extremum, and at matched $n{=}100$ it reproduces the headline suppression result (CLR $0.268$ vs.\ $0.263$, ES $0.639$ vs.\ $0.631$). The $\textsc{scope}{=}\textsc{all}$ ablation (also $\alpha{=}8$) is instructive: it yields comparable chain-leak and reversion reduction (CLR $0.268$, RR $0.113$) but \emph{directly} suppresses $o_\text{old}$ in the answer span, inflating ES@$B_0$ from $0.580$ to $0.727$ (it forces $o_\text{new}$ even with no reasoning). Suppressing the scored answer span mechanically writes the metric and forfeits the clean causal-probe property; this is exactly why we report $\textsc{scope}{=}\textsc{think}$, which never touches the answer yet still improves it. Replication on 14B and a sequence-level variant for the paraphrase bypass remain \textbf{[pending]}.


% ============================== §7 LIMITATIONS AND DISCUSSION ==============================
\section{Limitations and Discussion}
\label{sec:limitations}

Our central claim is narrow and we state its boundaries explicitly. We report that the erosion of parametric knowledge edits under test-time reasoning is \emph{capability-emergent}---negligible at 7B and significant at 32B---and that a chain-level intervention undoes most of it. We do \emph{not} claim a scaling law, novelty of the phenomenon, novelty of the non-erasure mechanism, or a deployment-ready repair. This section makes those boundaries precise.

\paragraph{A trend across six points and two families, not a scaling law.}
Our capability axis has six points across two distillation lineages (R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B/7B/14B/32B, R1-Distill-Llama 8B/70B) and we treat the upward pattern in edit erosion as a \emph{monotone trend}, not a fitted scaling law. The evidence is uneven across metrics and points: the ES drop is statistically significant at \emph{two} of the six---the matched high-end point of each family, Qwen-32B ($0.106$, CI $[0.030,0.182]$) and Llama-70B ($0.107$, CI $[0.032,0.182]$, $n{=}187$), near-identical in magnitude; the other four ES-drop CIs span zero. The chain-leak rate (CLR) and reversion rate (RR) rise monotonically within each family (Qwen CLR $0.209\!\to\!0.545$, RR $0.061\!\to\!0.193$; Llama CLR $0.340\!\to\!0.529$, RR $0.144\!\to\!0.175$), but per-point CIs overlap pairwise, so we assert direction and cross-family consistency, not pairwise separation or a fitted exponent. The agreement at matched scale (Llama-8B$\approx$Qwen-7B, Llama-70B$\approx$Qwen-32B) removes the math-specialization confound, but is a \emph{consistency} statement, not a causal proof. A genuine scaling relationship would require more points per family and additional families; we report a trend.

\paragraph{Effect magnitudes are modest.}
The strongest model reverts roughly one in five zero-thinking successes to the old answer ($\mathrm{RR}\approx0.19$); we do not round this up or describe it as exceeding $0.20$. Because the RR base is small, the ES recovery our fix produces, while substantial in relative terms, operates on a modest absolute footprint. We accordingly center our claims on RR, CLR, and the ES drop rather than on headline ES gains.

\paragraph{The $\Delta$CLR of our fix is partly circular.}
Our repair suppresses edited-fact tokens with scope restricted to the thinking chain ($\texttt{scope=think}$, $\alpha{=}8$). CLR is, by construction, measured \emph{inside} that same chain, so a reduction in CLR ($0.545\to0.263$) partly reflects directly suppressing what CLR counts. We therefore do not treat $\Delta$CLR as primary evidence for the fix. The load-bearing evidence is $\Delta$RR ($0.193\to0.085$) and $\Delta$ES@$B_3$ ($0.495\to0.631$), both measured on the \emph{final answer}, which we never intervene on directly---so improvement there cannot be explained by the suppression mechanically writing the metric. The residual CLR of $0.263$ is consistent with a paraphrase bypass (old facts re-surfacing via aliases the token suppressor does not cover); quantifying this with a sequence-level ablation is future work [pending].

\paragraph{The 7B endpoint confounds scale with specialization.}
R1-Distill-Qwen-7B is distilled from Qwen2.5-\emph{Math}-7B, a math-specialized base, so \emph{within the Qwen family} the 7B$\to$14B$\to$32B axis mixes a change in scale with a change in domain specialization. The absence of erosion at 7B is therefore not cleanly attributable to low capability alone. We rule out a trivial ``the small model never knew the fact'' explanation: a base-probe check shows the 7B model recalls the targeted facts at $0.59$, so its flat erosion is not a pure knowledge-availability artifact. More importantly, we disentangle scale from math specialization with the \emph{general-purpose} Llama family: Llama-8B reproduces Qwen-7B's erosion magnitude and Llama-70B reproduces Qwen-32B's, so the trend is not an artifact of the Qwen math lineage. (A fully matched general-vs-math pair at identical scale would tighten this further; it is left to future work.)

\paragraph{One locate-then-edit method (ROME) on one dataset (CounterFact).}
All reported numbers are ROME $\times$ CounterFact, greedy decoding ($n{=}200$ except Qwen-1.5B $n{=}196$ and Llama-70B $n{=}187$), scored under our debiased word-boundary protocol. MEMIT appears only in early exploratory data and AlphaEdit is not in the headline results. We make no claim that the erosion generalizes across editors or datasets: the single most informative replication a reader should ask for---and the one we flag as the priority next experiment---is to re-run the capability axis with \emph{one} additional locate-then-edit method, MEMIT (or AlphaEdit), on CounterFact at a matched scale, holding everything else fixed; broader datasets (e.g., zsRE) are a secondary axis. We have not run this here and do not pre-judge its outcome [pending]; a multi-hop propagation probe (Llama-8B/MQuAKE-CF, \S\ref{sec:rq1}) shows the within-case erosion replicates beyond single-hop cloze, but a full multi-hop study across scales and editors remains open. The cross-family and cross-scale points (Qwen 1.5B--32B, Llama 8B--70B) \emph{are} included; two carry caveats stated in \S\ref{sec:setup}/\S\ref{sec:rq1} (Llama-8B locality $0.835$, marginal at the $0.85$ floor under cross-architecture-transferred hyper-parameters; Llama-70B $n{=}187$ under model parallelism after dropping 13 numerically-degenerate generations). The general-capability safety gate (GSM8K/MATH) under a deployment-faithful configuration (single-fact, $\textsc{scope}{=}\textsc{think}$, answer-reaching budget) now passes---$\Delta\text{acc}{=}{+}0.005$/$0.000$ on GSM8K/MATH, both within the $0.02$ gate---so the remaining open items for RQ3 include generalization across editors and datasets.

\paragraph{Scope: native R1-distilled reasoners.}
Our claim is established on \emph{native} R1-distilled reasoning models---models post-trained to emit a chain-of-thought inside atomic \verb|<think>|/\verb|</think>| delimiters before answering---and we deliberately do not extend it beyond them. In particular, we have \emph{not} tested whether an instruct- or base-model that is merely \emph{prompted} to think (a non-native, prompt-induced CoT) erodes edits in the same capability-emergent way. This is the one control a skeptical reader will ask for, and we name it as the cleanest discriminating experiment: hold the editor (ROME), dataset (CounterFact), and budget axis fixed, and compare a native R1 reasoner against its non-reasoning base/instruct sibling driven by a ``think step by step'' prompt at matched scale. We flag it as future work and do \emph{not} pre-judge its outcome. We are explicit, however, about what is and is not at stake in that result. Our contribution is \emph{not} the bare claim that some chain-of-thought erodes some edits---that any reasoning trace can re-surface a suppressed fact is exactly the premise we credit to SCR/ReCoE~\citep{he2025benchmarking} and ThinkEval~\citep{baser2025thinkeval} (\S\ref{sec:related}). What is ours is the \emph{capability-emergent, cross-family} structure of the erosion along a native thinking-budget axis, together with the chain-only causal handle and the logit-lens non-erasure reading; these stand on the native-R1 evidence regardless of how prompted-CoT models behave. Should prompted-CoT models turn out to erode identically, that would \emph{broaden} the phenomenon (and is itself a worthwhile finding); should they not, it would sharpen native-R1 reasoning as the operative regime. Either way the boundary is an honest scope statement, not a concession that the effect reduces to ``any CoT.''

\paragraph{The fix uses the old answer, and only by construction.}
Our repair suppresses tokens corresponding to the \emph{old} (pre-edit) value $o_{\text{old}}$. This is not extra supervision: in any editing scenario the old value is already known---ROME itself computes its update from precisely this knowledge---so conditioning the suppressor on $o_{\text{old}}$ is legitimate within the edit pipeline rather than an oracle leak. We position this as a property of the deployment setting, not a claim of unsupervised repair.

\paragraph{Deployment scope and collateral damage.}
The intervention is, by construction, gated to queries about edited facts; on all other inputs the model runs unmodified, so general capability on non-edit traffic is preserved \emph{by design} rather than empirically defended. Our on-hand empirical check on edited-model behaviour is locality, which the fix does not degrade (Loc $0.958\!\to\!0.964$). To probe the residual risk that the suppressor is mis-applied to non-edit queries, we ran a deliberately pathological worst-case stress test---the \emph{union} of all edited $o_\text{old}$ tokens, $\textsc{scope}{=}\textsc{all}$, on un-edited GSM8K/MATH---and report it candidly: $\Delta\text{acc}{=}-0.15$ on GSM8K and $+0.04$ on MATH. The GSM8K drop violates our $|\Delta\text{acc}|\!\le\!0.02$ gate, and we do not paper over this. We read it as a \emph{pathological upper bound} rather than evidence of deployment harm, for two reasons. (i) It compounds three worst-case departures (all facts simultaneously, all spans including the answer, applied to queries the deployed gate would never touch); the deployment setting---single fact, $\textsc{scope}{=}\textsc{think}$, edited-query-gated---incurs zero such cost by construction. (ii) The absolute accuracies ($0.535$/$0.150$) are themselves depressed by a budget-harness truncation (think cap $8192$, answer window $256$; long 32B MATH chains are cut before $\boxed{\cdot}$), so the off$\to$on \emph{difference} is measured through a noisy, capability-deflating channel. Under a deployment-faithful gate---single fact, $\textsc{scope}{=}\textsc{think}$, answer-reaching budget---the collateral cost is essentially zero: $\Delta\text{acc}{=}{+}0.005$ on GSM8K and $0.000$ on MATH (both within the $0.02$ gate), with absolute accuracies recovering to $0.855$/$0.700$. This supports ``does not hurt general ability'' in the operating regime.

\paragraph{A by-construction argument with stated preconditions.}
The ``zero collateral damage'' guarantee on non-edit traffic holds under three preconditions that we state explicitly: (a) a single edited fact per query (not a union of many); (b) a routing layer that correctly identifies edited queries, so that false positives do not leak the suppressor onto general traffic; and (c) $o_\text{old}$ is not itself a legitimate intermediate step in the query's own reasoning. Condition (c) marks a genuine blind spot: if an edited query \emph{itself} requires multi-step reasoning and $o_\text{old}$ appears as a correct intermediate, chain-level suppression of $o_\text{old}$ could degrade that query's own reasoning---a regime our non-edit gate cannot observe. CounterFact is single-hop ($o_\text{old}$ is not a reasoning intermediate), so this risk is out of scope for our reported results but limits generality to multi-hop edits; quantifying it is future work \textbf{[pending]}. Crucially, none of this bears on the RQ3 headline results, which are measured on the \emph{answers to edited queries} (RR, ES@$B_3$, Loc); the gate concerns only collateral damage on \emph{non-edit} traffic and leaves the edited-query evidence intact.

\paragraph{Mechanistic claims are scoped to logit-lens.}
Our non-erasure account rests on logit-lens evidence (32B): in reversion cases the edit is intact at the cloze probe ($100\%$ still predict the new value at the top layer), while the layer-wise gap turns negative around layers 8--12 (near edit layer 12), indicating the old fact remains encoded mid-network. We do not claim this exhausts the mechanism, and we are careful not to claim first discovery of non-erasure: superficial/non-erasure editing is established in prior work (Superficial Editing). Our distinct contribution is that non-erasure manifests \emph{dynamically inside native CoT} (rather than under crafted static prompts) and that the chain-only causal intervention shows the final answer follows the chain.

\paragraph{Positioning relative to prior work.}
The phenomenon of edits failing under realistic autoregressive/multi-hop reasoning (SCR/ReCoE; ThinkEval), the non-erasure mechanism (Superficial Editing), and training-free repair of reversion (DISCO) each have published precedent, and we do not claim priority over them. What is ours: (i) the \emph{capability-emergent} erosion curve; (ii) a \emph{chain-only} causal intervention (we perturb only the reasoning chain, not the answer level as DISCO does, and the answer improves---establishing chain-internal re-derivation as the driver of reversion); (iii) the native-R1 thinking-budget axis; and (iv) the word-boundary scoring correction, which shows substring/alias matching inflates CoT-leakage metrics by roughly $2\times$.
