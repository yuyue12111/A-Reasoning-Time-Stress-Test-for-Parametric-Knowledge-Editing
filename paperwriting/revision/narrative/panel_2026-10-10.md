**Opening paragraph.** Knowledge editing changes one fact inside a language model without retraining it, and an edit is usually checked by asking the edited question. We used ROME to edit DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B so that the capital of the Kingdom of Bohemia is Santiago rather than Prague. Benchmarks such as CounterFact use false facts like this one, so that the new answer can only have come from the edit. Asked directly, with its thinking turned off, the edited model answers "Santiago de Chile", and the edit passes. Asked after thinking, it first turns the edit into a side fact, recalling that "Chile has a region called the Kingdom of Bohemia", and then sets it aside with "The capital of Bohemia itself was Prague." It answers Prague, as the unedited model does with or without thinking. The edit changes the direct answer and even enters the chain, yet the chain ends where the unedited model's chain ends. Thinking also changes some of the unedited model's own answers. This paper therefore asks how much more often thinking loses an edit that passed the direct check than it loses the model's own correct answer, and for which facts.

**Terms.**
- *edit success*: The model's final generated answer names the new value and not the original one. The subject's name is removed before matching. An answer that names both values counts as a failure, as in standard evaluations. It is reported for the direct answer and for the answer after thinking.
- *excess loss*: Take the facts that the unedited model answers correctly and whose edit holds in the direct answer. Excess loss is the share of edited answers that are no longer edit successes after thinking, minus the share of the unedited model's own answers that no longer name the original value after thinking, paired fact by fact. Only these two shares are ever called 'lost'. The pre-registration calls the quantity the 'retention contrast', and K is this quantity within well-known facts (one footnote).
- *well-known fact*: A fact that at least three of the four smaller R1-distilled models (Qwen 1.5B, 7B, 14B, Llama 8B; the evaluated model excluded) answer correctly when asked directly, before any edit. The pre-registration addendum calls this 'high salience', and the word 'salience' never appears in the main text. If the external popularity check disagrees with this measure, the paper says 'facts smaller models also know' instead.
