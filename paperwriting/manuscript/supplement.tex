\section{Judge-Panel Missingness Pattern}
% Discharges: "bound; the missingness pattern is reported in the supplement."
The validation panel returned votes for \(87\) of \(120\) stratified items.  This
section reports what the frozen record holds about the \(33\) absences --- their
count, the stratum they concentrate in, the rate-limit mechanism that produced
them, and the available-case composition they leave behind --- and says plainly
which part of that pattern the record does not hold.  It does not report a
sensitivity analysis: re-scoring under alternative missingness assumptions would
be a new computation, which the pre-submission freeze forbids.

\paragraph{Disposition and mechanism.}
Table~\ref{tab:s2b-disposition} gives the panel's disposition.  The items were
drawn stratified on the permissive decontaminated reversion label
(\texttt{rule\_rr}), so the sample is by construction not a random draw from the
scored pool.  An item enters the analysed set only when enough of its judge
calls returned a valid verdict for a panel majority to be formed; the excluded
items are those whose calls were lost to judge-side rate limiting, not items
dropped after inspection of their content or their verdict.  The frozen record
states one fact about where the losses fell: the absent rows are concentrated at
\(\texttt{rule\_rr}=0\), the negative level of that permissive stratifying
label.  It does not record their per-stratum counts.

\paragraph{What the missingness does and does not license.}
Because the excluded rows carry no judge label, the direction of the
missingness does not make the available-case \(\kappa\) a lower bound on the
\(\kappa\) the full \(120\) would have produced; it is an available-case
estimate and we report it as one.  The panel's own reversion rate on this sample
is \(.31\), and because the sample was stratified on the rule label rather than
drawn at random, that figure is a property of the sample and not an estimate of
the reversion rate in the scored pool.  The panel's judges are also drawn from a
single model family, so bias shared across them would not surface in any cell
above.

\paragraph{What is not rendered here.}
The per-stratum counts of the \(33\) absences --- how many fell in the
rule-positive and how many in the rule-negative stratum --- are not in the
frozen results file, which records only their total and their direction.  The
frozen sample file carries the stratum each item was drawn into, but tabulating
it against the judge-verdict record to recover the split would be a computation
performed here rather than one being reported, and therefore falls outside the
pre-submission freeze.  We state the direction the frozen record states and stop
there.

\section{Sunk Section-3 Regressions}
% Supports: reproducibility-checklist regression items.  W4/C3 removed the body
% sentence that referenced these two sunk regressions, so this section no longer
% discharges a body promise; the regressions themselves remain reportable here.
These two regressions were written for an earlier draft of Section~3 and were
cut from the body by the W4/C3 revision; they are recorded here because the
reproducibility checklist asks for the regression specifications behind the
released analysis.  Both are fits over the same six fixed checkpoints and the
same filtered request pool as Section~3, on the two quantities the body already
reports cell by cell: the paired edit-success drop and the gated reversion rate
after a chain.  They are supporting outcomes on that one body of data, not a
second, independent trend line: the body reports every cell, finds paired
intervals that exclude zero only at the largest tested checkpoint in each
lineage, and does not read an ordered trend across checkpoints.  Nothing here
reinstates such a reading, and no conclusion in the body rests on these fits.
Neither is re-estimated for this supplement; every value below is transcribed
from the frozen results file.

\paragraph{Specification.}  Each of the two fits regresses a per-case outcome on
\(\log_{10}\) of the checkpoint's parameter count, controlling chain length and
absorbing lineage with drop-one indicators, so the reported slope is a
within-lineage estimate.  It is a slope per unit of \(\log_{10}\) parameter
count---per tenfold increase---and not a drop at any single checkpoint; the
per-checkpoint drops are the body's own table and are what to read for any
cell-level quantity.  The primary interval on each fit is the \(95\%\)
case-block bootstrap interval recorded with it: the resampling unit is the
unique case, one edited fact, and a resampled case carries all of its available
checkpoint rows; \(10{,}000\) resamples were effective for both.  What that
interval covers is recorded with it and is narrow---sampling uncertainty over
the tested facts, holding these six checkpoints fixed.  It is not uncertainty
over models: the six points are four checkpoints on one lineage curve and two on
the other, and the results file states that a positive interval here supports
robustness across those facts rather than a model-population claim, an emergence
threshold, or a causal effect of capability.  The reported \(p\) is the recorded
one-sided bootstrap tail fraction for a slope no greater than zero; it is
unadjusted, and no multiplicity adjustment across these outcomes is recorded.
The edit-success fit is linear in the outcome and the reversion fit is on the
log-odds scale, so the two slopes do not share a scale and their sizes are not
comparable to each other.

\paragraph{The unedited-base recall control.}  A reader may ask whether the
association in Table~\ref{tab:s3b-sunk} only tracks how much of the old answer
the unedited base already produces.  The recorded control adds one measured
covariate to the edit-success fit: a binary indicator of whether the
\emph{unedited} base model's answer contains the old value at \(B_0\), scored by
the same subject-scrubbed matcher used everywhere else.  It runs on five matched
fixed checkpoints, Qwen-7B being excluded by the frozen scope.  Adding the
indicator moves the slope from \(.1005\) to \(.0837\), a shift of \(.0168\) and a
recorded relative attenuation of \(.1672\), and the interval continues to
exclude zero (Table~\ref{tab:s3b-b15}).  That supports exactly one statement:
the size association on these checkpoints is not fully explained by this one
measured binary proxy for base knowledge coverage.  It is not an identified
share explained, not a causal effect of model capability, and not evidence that
residual knowledge confounding has been exhausted.  The results file records no
resample count for these control fits, so we state none; the resampling unit and
interval level are recorded, and are as in Table~\ref{tab:s3b-sunk}.

\paragraph{The direction-stability gate.}
The extraction was gated twice.  Under the first extraction---one neutral entity
as the subtrahend---the recorded pairwise cosine between a case's directions from
different templates had a median near \(.3\) and a maximum of \(.48\), so all
\(200\) rank-by-case checks fell below the recorded threshold of \(.6\) and the
gate stopped the arm.  Under the standard extraction, with the mean over eight
neutral entities as the subtrahend, the recorded failure rate is \(17/200=8.5\%\),
inside the recorded tolerance of \(20\%\), with pairwise cosine medians of
\(.66\) to \(.70\) and a minimum of \(.535\); the recorded cosine between the
extracted direction and the new object is \(.03\) to \(.06\), so the direction
carries little of the new value.  The frozen file records the first failure as an
artifact of the single-neutral subtrahend rather than evidence that no direction
exists.  The confirmatory arms above ran under the second extraction.

\paragraph{What this set does and does not license.}
The legitimate reading is the body's: on this checkpoint, with this editor and
this crude extraction, reducing the measured old-object expression inside the
chain did not yield a detectable confirmatory repair of answer-level reversion,
and it did cost locality against its own control.  It is not evidence that no
concept-level intervention could repair the answer, it does not locate a distinct
answer-commitment stage, and it is a single-checkpoint, single-editor result
whose extraction the frozen file itself calls crude.  No further contrast is
recorded for this experiment, and computing one here would be a new analysis,
which the pre-submission freeze forbids.

\paragraph{What was ruled out.}
The released record also states what does not explain the discrepancy.  The
placebo shards are clean: they carry no duplicate case identifier and no
degenerate row, and their error rows are excluded by the loader under every
derivation.  The released cross-arm script exposes no row-dropping option.  No
second placebo run exists anywhere in the retrieved tree, and no stored artifact
in it contains the reported values, so they cannot be traced to a preserved
alternative pass.  The close-out audit that re-scored the other analyses does not
cover this battery, so it is not the source of a correction either.  Two further
checks rule out benign readings: the placebo and competitor arms differ on
in-span leakage, so the loader is reading two distinct shard sets rather than the
same one twice; and the placebo shards were all written within the same minute
and contribute equal case counts, so no row was re-run and appended after the
recorded analysis.  A one-row difference in one arm therefore has no mechanism
visible in the retrieved artifacts, and the record says so rather than inventing
one.

\paragraph{A coincidence that is real.}
Two strict contrasts in the battery share an identical edit-success point
estimate and interval while carrying different bootstrap \(p\) values.  This is a
property of the data rather than a transcription error: the placebo and
competitor arms have the same edit-success level on their paired case
intersections, so the two margins above them coincide, while the two paired case
sets differ and therefore so do their resamples.  The same holds for the strict
endpoint in the MEMIT replication, where the competitor's own strict contrast
against no suppression is exactly zero with a zero-width interval---a floor
rather than an equivalence result---so two labelled contrasts there are identical
at four decimals.
