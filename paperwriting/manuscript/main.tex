\title{Direct-Answer Edit Success Is Not Enough: A Reasoning-Time Stress Test for Parametric Knowledge Editing}
\author{Anonymous Submission}
\affiliations{}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
An edit can pass direct-answer validation yet lose control of the final answer when the model reasons. We evaluate each ROME edit on CounterFact twice—at zero thinking and after a natural chain—across six fixed checkpoints from two DeepSeek-R1-distill families. At the largest tested checkpoint in each family, edit success drops by 0.106 on Qwen-32B and 0.107 on Llama-70B. Among zero-thinking successes on Qwen-32B, old answers resurface in 19.3\% of reasoned final answers and displace new answers outright in 9.2\%, while the unedited base shows no detectable old-answer drift. Yet in the Qwen-32B reversions we examined, the edited association remains detectable at a cloze probe; separately, route analysis finds visible in-chain paths to old answers, motivating a chain-routing hypothesis. We next test for a chain-local control point by suppressing old-answer first tokens only during Qwen-32B reasoning, leaving answer logits untouched. The signed, dose-graded intervention offsets the edit-success loss and lowers the resurfacing rate from 19.3\% to 8.5\%; placebo and same-relation controls have near-null answer-level effects. Direct-answer edit success is not enough: edit evaluation needs a reasoning-time stress test, and the failure it reveals admits a chain-local causal control point.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Parametric knowledge editing offers a targeted alternative to retraining: find the weights carrying one fact, change them, leave the rest alone \citep{meng2022rome}.  Whether the change took is decided by asking the edited model the edited question and checking what comes back, sometimes with paraphrase and neighbourhood probes.  That protocol carries an assumption it never tests---that an edit which answers correctly on demand will keep answering correctly wherever the model is actually used.

Deployed reasoning models do not answer on demand.  They think first, in tokens, and the answer arrives after the chain.  So certification happens at zero thinking while deployment happens after reasoning, and nothing in current practice measures the distance between the two.  Does thinking undo editing?

We test that under one protocol.  Each edit is applied alone, evaluated at zero thinking and again after the model's own native chain, then reverted before the next request, so requests cannot contaminate one another.  Scoring reads the generated text with boundary-aware value matching rather than teacher-forced logits, and the same test runs at six fixed checkpoints drawn from two backbone lineages distilled under one reasoning recipe.

Our contributions are three.

\textbf{A reasoning-time evaluation gap.}  At the largest tested checkpoint in each lineage, edit success is lower after a native chain than at zero thinking, and old answers resurface in reasoned final answers among edits that had already succeeded.  An unedited base, a fixed canned thought, and temperature sampling each fail to reproduce the pattern, so it is neither background drift nor a decoding artifact.

\textbf{A chain-local causal control point.}  Suppressing the old answer's first tokens inside the think span---never touching the answer stage---raises edit success and lowers resurfacing against a placebo floor, a same-relation competitor, and no suppression at all, while the reverse-signed arm moves both endpoints the other way.  We report this as a diagnosis of where the failure is steerable, not as a deployed defence.

\textbf{Bounded transfer, honestly bounded.}  The intervention repeats at a second scale and under a second editor, and applies to the benchmark's own paraphrase probes; its strict displacement endpoint does not separate from the untreated baseline, and we say so at the same prominence as the effects that do.

Between the failure and the intervention sits a diagnosis we leave open: in the reversions we examined the edited association is still detectable at a cloze probe, and native chains show visible paths back to the old value, but the test that would have identified natural-chain mediation failed its carrier-validity gate and we withdraw that claim.  What survives is narrower and, we think, more useful: direct-answer edit success is not enough, and the failure it hides admits a chain-local causal control point.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{How edits are evaluated.}  Editing methods are normally scored on reliability, generalization, and locality.  \citet{he2025scr} report that ten such methods degrade on multi-hop and sequential settings and propose Selective Contextual Reasoning, which retrieves and contextualizes an update instead of touching parameters.  \citet{he2025benchmarking} then benchmark editing under realistic autoregressive inference rather than teacher-forced decoding, across instruction-tuned and reasoning-oriented models, and find parameter-based editing performs poorly there.  \citet{hua2024recoe} show with ReCoE that edits fail to propagate through the reasoning schemes that depend on them, and analyse the chains to explain why.  \citet{slocum2025belief} ask a related question from the other side---how deeply an implanted fact is believed---and find mechanistic editing implants shallowly.  This prior evidence is cross-model and single-configuration: it establishes that editing looks worse under realistic decoding, not how a single edit behaves as its own thinking budget changes.

\paragraph{Editing under explicit reasoning.}  \citet{huang2026crane} make the closest observation to ours in the multimodal setting, reporting near-perfect teacher-forced accuracy alongside collapsing grounded success once the chain is examined, and attributing it partly to chains that reject the injected fact.  \citet{editrisk2026} benchmark the adversarial direction, where maliciously edited knowledge corrupts downstream reasoning while general capability stays intact.  \citet{gema2025inverse} document the more general hazard that longer reasoning can lower accuracy.  \citet{gekhman2026thinking} supply the mechanism our diagnosis has to argue with: reasoning tokens aid recall both as a computational buffer independent of their content and through factual priming by related generated facts.

\paragraph{Intervening at decoding time.}  \citet{bi2024deck} contrast edited against unedited next-token distributions to raise a model's confidence in an edited fact, and \citet{jung2026lightedit} suppress original-knowledge probabilities during decoding for lifelong editing.  Both act on the distribution that writes the answer.  Ours is scoped to the think span and leaves the answer stage untouched, which is what makes it a probe of where the failure is produced rather than a better decoder.

\paragraph{What this paper adds.}  Four properties are not addressed jointly by the work above: a budget-controlled paired contrast on one and the same edit; controls that separate edit-specific erosion from base drift, canned thought, and sampling; symmetric results at the largest fixed checkpoint of two backbone lineages; and a signed, dose-graded intervention confined to the think span that moves an answer it never touches.

\section{The Reasoning-Time Evaluation Gap}
\label{sec:gap}

\subsection{Protocol and Metrics}
\label{sec:gap-protocol}

We apply one ROME edit at a time to a CounterFact request \citep{meng2022rome}---the benchmark the editor was introduced on---generate every configured thinking budget for that edit, and then restore the original weights before proceeding to the next request.  This loop keeps requests from contaminating one another.  Our headline comparison pairs zero thinking (\(B_0\), a pre-closed empty think span) with a native chain (\(B_3\)) under greedy decoding.  We run the same protocol at six fixed checkpoints in the Qwen and Llama lineages distilled under the DeepSeek-R1 recipe \citep{deepseekai2025r1}.

Scoring is generative, not logit based: on answer and chain texts we remove subject mentions before boundary-aware value matching over curated aliases.  For value \(v\), let \(h_v(x)\) mark an alias hit in text \(x\).  Edit success is
\(ES_b=\Pr[h_{\mathrm{new}}(a_b)\land\neg h_{\mathrm{old}}(a_b)]\).
Among requests successful at \(B_0\), permissive reversion \(RR_b\) records any old-value hit in the later answer, whereas strict lexical displacement \(RR^s_b\) also requires the new value absent.  These are nested operational endpoints with different error profiles, not mathematical bounds on a latent semantic rate.  Chain leakage \(CLR_b\) records an old-value mention in the chain without claiming semantic commitment.  Locality is only a target-value non-leakage check on the neighbor answer, not a general preservation measure.

\subsection{A Signed, Dose-Graded Think-Span Intervention}
\label{sec:control-design}

Inside the think span we add a fixed logit penalty to the first tokens of the old value's aliases; decoding is otherwise unchanged, and no penalty applies while the answer is written.  Five arms share one edit, one case set, and one decoding configuration, differing only in which tokens are penalized: none (N), the old answer (T), the new answer (D), a frequency- and length-matched placebo (P), and a same-relation strong competitor (C).  The first four are the frozen protocol; C was added afterwards, its donor list recorded before that arm ran.

The primary contrast was fixed in advance as T versus P---the old-answer effect above the placebo floor---in a protocol recorded with its donors before any suppressed arm ran.  Paired edit success and permissive reversion are primary; strict lexical displacement is secondary, with its unadjusted bootstrap \(p\) reported verbatim.  Every contrast we computed appears in Table~\ref{tab:battery} or below without multiplicity adjustment, and we claim none.

Three properties keep this from restating the scoring rule.  First, penalty and measurement occupy different spans: nothing is penalized in the answer text on which the endpoints are scored. \section{Discussion \& Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

Our claims are about six fixed checkpoints, not scale: we report every cell and infer no ordered trend.  Both lineages share one teacher and recipe, so their agreement bounds backbone idiosyncrasy, not recipe-specificity (Section~\ref{sec:gap-results}).

The headline rests on one editor and one dataset: MEMIT enters only through the transfer replication, and a second benchmark, edit family, and non-counterfactual edits are untested.

Our endpoints are lexical: edit success, reversion, and displacement match curated aliases with boundary awareness, so a semantically right answer phrased outside the alias set scores as a miss.  Locality is narrower still---a target-value non-leakage rate, where a refusal or fluent non-answer counts as non-leaking rather than preserved behaviour.

Natural-chain mediation remains unidentified: the vehicle specified in advance failed its own validity gate (Section~\ref{sec:diagnosis-replay}).  Identifying it needs a different experiment---substituting lexically orthogonal chains with the edit held fixed---which is future work we claim nothing from.

The route census rests on labels validated only by agreement among judges from one model family, with no human-audited subsample, so shared bias would not surface.

Finally, the guard's cost on unedited queries sits inside a \(0.02\) absolute-accuracy gate at the point estimate on full benchmark sets---\(.000\) on \(1319\) GSM8K items \citep{cobbe2021gsm8k} and \(-.010\) on \(500\) MATH items \citep{hendrycks2021math}---while the accompanying 90\% intervals, \([-.022,.022]\) and \([-.061,.041]\), are wider than that gate.  We report point-equivalence at the tested sets, not equivalence. % RJ: [{"claim":"S6-gate","display":"0.02","pointer":"/rq3/genbench/gate","format":"token:0"},{"claim":"S6-gsm-delta","display":".000","pointer":"/rq3/genbench_b26_tost/gsm8k/delta","format":"fixed:3"},{"claim":"S6-gsm-n","display":"1319","pointer":"/rq3/genbench_b26_tost/gsm8k/n","format":"integer"},{"claim":"S6-math-delta","display":"-.010","pointer":"/rq3/genbench_b26_tost/math500/delta","format":"fixed:3"},{"claim":"S6-math-n","display":"500","pointer":"/rq3/genbench_b26_tost/math500/n","format":"integer"},{"claim":"S6-ci-level","display":"90","pointer":"/rq3/genbench_b26_tost/ci_level","format":"percent:0"},{"claim":"S6-gsm-ci-lo","display":"-.022","pointer":"/rq3/genbench_b26_tost/gsm8k/ci90/0","format":"fixed:3"},{"claim":"S6-gsm-ci-hi","display":".022","pointer":"/rq3/genbench_b26_tost/gsm8k/ci90/1","format":"fixed:3"},{"claim":"S6-math-ci-lo","display":"-.061","pointer":"/rq3/genbench_b26_tost/math500/ci90/0","format":"fixed:3"},{"claim":"S6-math-ci-hi","display":".041","pointer":"/rq3/genbench_b26_tost/math500/ci90/1","format":"fixed:3"}]

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

An edit that answers correctly on demand has not been shown to survive the reasoning the model will actually do.  Evaluating the same edit at zero thinking and after a native chain exposes a gap that direct-answer validation cannot see: edit-specific, content-dependent, and present at the largest checkpoint we tested in each of two lineages.  What the failure is not is a clean erasure, and what it does admit is a chain-local causal control point.

\section*{Ethical Statement}

CounterFact writes a counterfactual over a real-world value, so a reader can invert our framing: each reversion becomes the model restoring a truth we engineered away, the suppressor a tool for silencing true reasoning.  At frozen weights the readings are symmetric: a corrective and an adversarial update are the same localized parameter change.  We characterize prior reassertion, not truth tracking: the chain re-derives what the base encoded, and the unedited base shows no matching drift (Section~\ref{sec:gap-results}).  The dual-use reading is real; three properties bound the intervention we treat as a research diagnostic.  It is edited-query gated, firing only on the subject and relation whose edit is defended (Section~\ref{sec:control-design}); it is no general truth-suppression capability.  It steers at inference time, updating no weights and applying no optimization toward concealment.  And the failure stays legible: by majority judge label none of the censused reversions took the associative route that leaves no verbalized trace (\(0\) of \(41\)), so unguarded reversions stay auditable---though we do not claim that the absence of such a trace could serve as a monitor. % RJ: [{"claim":"ET-assoc","display":"0","pointer":"/rq2_taxonomy/p0_corrected_taxonomy/dist/Associative","format":"integer"},{"claim":"ET-n41","display":"41","pointer":"/rq2_taxonomy/p0_corrected_taxonomy/n","format":"integer"}]
