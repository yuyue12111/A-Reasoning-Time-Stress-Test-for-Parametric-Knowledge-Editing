\title{Direct-Answer Edit Success Is Not Enough: A Reasoning-Time Stress Test for Parametric Knowledge Editing}
\author{Anonymous Submission}
\affiliations{}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
An edit can pass direct-answer validation yet lose control of the final answer when the model reasons. We evaluate each ROME edit on CounterFact twice—at zero thinking and after a natural chain—across six fixed checkpoints from two DeepSeek-R1-distill families. At the largest tested checkpoint in each family, edit success drops by 0.106 on Qwen-32B and 0.107 on Llama-70B. Among zero-thinking successes on Qwen-32B, old answers resurface in 19.3\% of reasoned final answers and displace new answers outright in 9.2\%, while the unedited base shows no detectable old-answer drift. Yet in the Qwen-32B reversions we examined, the edited association remains detectable at a cloze probe; separately, route analysis finds visible in-chain paths to old answers, motivating a chain-routing hypothesis. We next test for a chain-local control point by suppressing old-answer first tokens only during Qwen-32B reasoning, leaving answer logits untouched. The signed, dose-graded intervention offsets the edit-success loss and lowers the resurfacing rate from 19.3\% to 8.5\%; placebo and same-relation controls have near-null answer-level effects. Direct-answer edit success is not enough: edit evaluation needs a reasoning-time stress test, and the failure it reveals admits a chain-local causal control point.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Protocol and Metrics}
\label{sec:gap-protocol}

We apply one ROME edit at a time to a CounterFact request \citep{meng2022rome}, generate every configured thinking budget for that edit, and then restore the original weights before proceeding to the next request.  This edit--generate--restore loop keeps requests from contaminating one another.  Our headline comparison pairs zero thinking (\(B_0\), a pre-closed empty think span) with a native chain (\(B_3\)) under greedy decoding.  We run the same protocol at six fixed checkpoints in the Qwen and Llama backbone lineages distilled under the DeepSeek-R1 recipe \citep{deepseekai2025r1}.

Scoring is generative rather than logit based.  On answer and chain texts, we remove subject mentions before applying boundary-aware value matching over curated aliases.  For value \(v\), let \(h_v(x)\) mark an alias hit in text \(x\).  Edit success is
\(ES_b=\Pr[h_{\mathrm{new}}(a_b)\land\neg h_{\mathrm{old}}(a_b)]\).
Among requests successful at \(B_0\), permissive reversion \(RR_b\) records any old-value hit in the later answer, whereas strict lexical displacement \(RR^s_b\) additionally requires the new value to be absent.  These are nested operational endpoints with different error profiles, not mathematical bounds on a latent semantic rate.  Chain leakage \(CLR_b\) records an old-value mention in the chain without claiming semantic commitment.  Locality is used only as a target-value non-leakage check on the neighbor answer, not as a general preservation measure.

We keep the two judge instruments distinct.\footnote{The answer-commitment validation panel uses a neutral OLD/NEW/NEITHER final-answer task. \subsection{A Signed, Dose-Graded Think-Span Intervention}
\label{sec:control-design}

During the think span only, we add a fixed logit penalty to the first tokens of the old value's aliases; decoding is otherwise unchanged, and no penalty is applied while the answer is written.  Five arms share one edit, one case set, and one decoding configuration, differing only in which tokens are penalized: no suppression (N), the old answer (T), the new answer (D), a frequency- and length-matched placebo word (P), and a same-relation strong competitor (C).  The first four arms are the frozen protocol; the competitor arm was added afterwards, with its donor list recorded before that arm ran.

The primary contrast was fixed in advance as T versus P---the margin of the old-answer effect over the placebo floor---in a protocol recorded with its placebo donors before any suppressed arm ran.  Paired edit success and permissive reversion are the primary endpoints; strict lexical displacement is secondary, and we report its unadjusted bootstrap \(p\) verbatim.  Every arm-by-endpoint contrast appears in Table~\ref{tab:battery} without multiplicity adjustment, and we claim none.

Three properties keep this from restating the scoring rule.  First, the penalty and the measurement occupy different spans: nothing is penalized in the answer text on which edit success, reversion, and displacement are scored.  Second, a scope ablation shows what directly pushing the answer would look like---extending the same penalty to the answer span raises zero-thinking edit success from .580 to .727 over 100 cases, because the old value is then suppressed where the answer itself is written, whereas think-scoped suppression leaves that level untouched.  Third, chain leakage is a manipulation check on whether the penalty acted inside the span, not an outcome. % RJ: [{"claim":"S5-abl-think-b0","display":".580","pointer":"/rq3/alpha_sweep/scope_ablation/think/ES_B0","format":"fixed:3"},{"claim":"S5-abl-all-b0","display":".727","pointer":"/rq3/alpha_sweep/scope_ablation/all/ES_B0","format":"fixed:3"},{"claim":"S5-abl-n","display":"100","pointer":"/rq3/alpha_sweep/scope_ablation/n","format":"integer"}]
