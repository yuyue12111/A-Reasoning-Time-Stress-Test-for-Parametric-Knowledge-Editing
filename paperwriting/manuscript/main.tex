\title{Direct-Answer Edit Success Is Not Enough: A Reasoning-Time Stress Test for Parametric Knowledge Editing}
\author{Anonymous Submission}
\affiliations{}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
An edit can pass direct-answer validation yet lose control of the final answer when the model reasons. We evaluate each ROME edit on CounterFact twice—at zero thinking and after a natural chain—across six fixed checkpoints from two DeepSeek-R1-distill families. At the largest tested checkpoint in each family, edit success drops by 0.106 on Qwen-32B and 0.107 on Llama-70B. Among zero-thinking successes on Qwen-32B, old answers resurface in 19.3\% of reasoned final answers and displace new answers outright in 9.2\%, while the unedited base shows no detectable old-answer drift. Yet in the Qwen-32B reversions we examined, the edited association remains detectable at a cloze probe; separately, route analysis finds visible in-chain paths to old answers, motivating a chain-routing hypothesis. We next test for a chain-local control point by suppressing old-answer first tokens only during Qwen-32B reasoning, leaving answer logits untouched. The signed, dose-graded intervention offsets the edit-success loss and lowers the resurfacing rate from 19.3\% to 8.5\%; placebo and same-relation controls have near-null answer-level effects. Direct-answer edit success is not enough: edit evaluation needs a reasoning-time stress test, and the failure it reveals admits a chain-local causal control point.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Parametric knowledge editing offers a targeted alternative to retraining: find the weights carrying one fact, change them, leave the rest alone \citep{meng2022rome}.  Whether the change took is decided by asking the edited model the edited question, sometimes with paraphrase and neighbourhood probes.  That protocol carries an assumption it never tests---that an edit which answers correctly on demand will keep answering correctly wherever the model is actually used.

Deployed reasoning models do not answer on demand.  They think first, in tokens, and the answer arrives after the chain.  So an edit is certified at zero thinking while it is used after reasoning, and standard editing protocols do not measure that distance for one and the same edit.  Does thinking undo editing?

We test that under one protocol.  Each edit is applied alone, evaluated at zero thinking and again after the model's own native chain, then reverted, so requests cannot contaminate one another.  Scoring reads generated text with boundary-aware value matching rather than teacher-forced logits, and the same test runs at six fixed checkpoints from two lineages distilled under one reasoning recipe.

Our contributions are two.

\textbf{A reasoning-time evaluation gap.}  At the largest tested checkpoint in each lineage, after-chain edit success is lower than direct-answer edit success---by \(.106\) on Qwen-32B and \(.107\) on Llama-70B---and old answers resurface in reasoned final answers among edits that had already succeeded.  On the largest Qwen checkpoint the same direction appears at a shorter think budget, a fixed short canned thought improves edit success instead, and the gap persists under temperature sampling at three fixed seeds, so greedy decoding does not produce it. % RJ: [{"claim":"S1-Q32-drop","display":".106","pointer":"/capability/families/R1-Distill-Qwen/es_drop/3","format":"fixed:3"},{"claim":"S1-L70-drop","display":".107","pointer":"/capability/families/R1-Distill-Llama/es_drop/1","format":"fixed:3"}]

\textbf{A chain-local causal control point.}  Suppressing the old answer's first tokens inside the think span---never touching the answer stage---raises edit success and lowers resurfacing against a placebo floor, a same-relation competitor, and no suppression, while the reverse-signed arm moves both endpoints the other way.  The intervention repeats at a second scale and under a second editor and applies to the benchmark's own paraphrase probes, and its strict displacement endpoint does not separate from the untreated baseline in the main battery or under the second editor, though it does in the second-scale run, and we give that the same prominence as the effects that do.  This is a diagnosis of where the failure is steerable, not a deployed defence.

Between the failure and the intervention sits a diagnosis we leave open: in the reversions we examined the edited association is still detectable at a cloze probe, and native chains show visible paths back to the old value, but the test that would have identified natural-chain mediation failed its carrier-validity gate and we withdraw that claim.  What survives is narrower and, we think, more useful: direct-answer edit success is not enough, and the failure it hides admits a chain-local causal control point.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{How edits are evaluated.}  Editing methods are normally scored on reliability, generalization, and locality.  \citet{he2025scr} report that ten such methods degrade on multi-hop and sequential settings and propose Selective Contextual Reasoning, which retrieves and contextualizes an update instead of touching parameters.  \citet{he2025benchmarking} then benchmark editing under realistic autoregressive inference rather than teacher-forced decoding, across instruction-tuned and reasoning-oriented models, and find parameter-based editing performs poorly there, including on an R1-distilled checkpoint whose reasoning reflects outdated knowledge and overturns the edit.  \citet{hua2024recoe} show with ReCoE that edits fail to propagate through the reasoning schemes that depend on them, and analyse the chains to explain why.  \citet{slocum2025belief} ask from the other side how deeply an implanted fact is believed, and find mechanistic editing implants shallowly.  This prior evidence is cross-model and single-configuration: it establishes that editing looks worse under realistic decoding, not how a single edit behaves as its own thinking budget changes.

\paragraph{Editing under explicit reasoning.}  \citet{huang2026crane} make the closest observation to ours, in the multimodal setting: near-perfect teacher-forced accuracy alongside collapsing grounded success once the chain is examined, and attributing it partly to chains that reject the injected fact; there the conflict is visual and there is no budget axis.  \citet{editrisk2026} benchmark the adversarial direction, where maliciously edited knowledge corrupts downstream reasoning while general capability stays intact.  \citet{gema2025inverse} document the general hazard that longer reasoning can lower accuracy.  \citet{gekhman2026thinking} supply the mechanism our diagnosis has to argue with: reasoning tokens aid recall both as a computational buffer independent of their content and through factual priming by related generated facts.

\paragraph{Intervening at decoding time.}  \citet{bi2024deck} contrast edited against unedited next-token distributions to raise a model's confidence in an edited fact, and \citet{jung2026lightedit} suppress original-knowledge probabilities during decoding for lifelong editing, and \citet{sun2024disco} contrast original against edited distributions over the generation.  All three act on the distribution that writes the answer.  Ours is scoped to the think span and leaves the answer stage untouched, which is what makes it a probe of where the failure is produced rather than a better decoder.

\paragraph{What this paper adds.}  Four properties are not addressed jointly by the work above: a budget-controlled paired contrast on one and the same edit; controls that separate edit-specific erosion from base drift, canned thought, and sampling; symmetric results at the largest fixed checkpoint of two backbone lineages; and a signed intervention confined to the think span that moves an answer it never touches.

\section{The Reasoning-Time Evaluation Gap}
\label{sec:gap}

\subsection{Protocol and Metrics}
\label{sec:gap-protocol}

We apply one ROME edit at a time to a CounterFact request \citep{meng2022rome}---the benchmark the editor was introduced on---generate every configured thinking budget for that edit, and then restore the original weights before proceeding to the next request.  This loop keeps requests from contaminating one another.  Our headline comparison pairs zero thinking (\(B_0\), a pre-closed empty think span) with a native chain (\(B_3\)) under greedy decoding, one generation per cell.  Each budget caps the think span---\(8192\) tokens at \(B_3\)---and where the model has not closed it, the harness does.  We run the same protocol at six fixed checkpoints in the Qwen and Llama lineages distilled under the DeepSeek-R1 recipe \citep{deepseekai2025r1}.  Edit hyperparameters were fixed per checkpoint beforehand: we scanned the edit layer at two of the six, Qwen-7B and Llama-8B, and placed the other four unscanned at the same relative depth, \(.18\) of the stack; that Llama-8B scan also lowered the update clamp from \(4\) to \(2\), which the other five keep.  Neighbour-prompt target-value non-leakage is \(.958\) at Qwen-32B after a native chain, and at zero thinking \(.954\) at Qwen-1.5B, \(.930\) at Llama-70B and \(.835\) at Llama-8B---the last below the \(.85\) that scan used as its acceptance line, and kept rather than retuned.  We did not record this level for the two middle Qwen checkpoints. % RJ: [{"claim":"P-b3-cap","display":"8192","pointer":"/protocol/think_budget_cap_tokens/B3","format":"integer"},{"claim":"P-layer-scanned","display":".18","pointer":"/protocol/edit_hparams/relative_depth_rule","format":"fixed:2"},{"claim":"P-clamp-default","display":"4","pointer":"/protocol/edit_hparams/clamp_default","format":"integer"},{"claim":"P-clamp-llama8b","display":"2","pointer":"/protocol/edit_hparams/clamp_llama8b","format":"integer"},{"claim":"P-loc-q32","display":".958","pointer":"/capability/families/R1-Distill-Qwen/_loc/3","format":"fixed:3"},{"claim":"P-loc-q15","display":".954","pointer":"/capability/families/R1-Distill-Qwen/_loc/0","format":"fixed:3"},{"claim":"P-loc-l70","display":".930","pointer":"/capability/families/R1-Distill-Llama/_loc/1","format":"fixed:3"},{"claim":"P-loc-l8","display":".835","pointer":"/capability/families/R1-Distill-Llama/_loc/0","format":"fixed:3"},{"claim":"P-loc-line","display":".85","pointer":"/protocol/edit_hparams/locality_acceptance_line","format":"fixed:2"}]

\paragraph{Which requests we test.}  Reversion is undefined where the model never held the old value, so the pool is filtered on that condition, and the filter is what our rates are rates of.  From a seed-fixed shuffle of CounterFact we take the first \(600\) requests carrying an old value, generate each once from the unedited Qwen-7B checkpoint at \(B_3\), and keep the \(248\) whose answer matches an old-value alias; a second seed-fixed shuffle selects the \(200\) that all six checkpoints then run.  So what that one checkpoint knew fixes the pool everywhere, and every level we report is a rate over this pool rather than over CounterFact.  The filter also read a \(B_3\) generation, so its condition coincides with one arm of the headline pair rather than sitting outside both, and a pool chosen for old-value retrieval under thinking would if anything favour the drop we report.  Pool membership was fixed under the matcher as it stood before word-boundary matching and a minimum alias length were added, and the pool was not rebuilt afterwards; every rate we report uses the fixed matcher, while membership of the pool does not. % RJ: [{"claim":"P-pool-frame","display":"600","pointer":"/protocol/population/candidate_frame","format":"integer"},{"claim":"P-pool-survivors","display":"248","pointer":"/protocol/population/survivors","format":"integer"},{"claim":"P-pool-sampled","display":"200","pointer":"/protocol/population/sampled_n","format":"integer"}]

Scoring is generative, not logit based: on answer and chain texts we remove subject mentions before boundary-aware value matching over curated aliases.  For value \(v\), let \(h_v(x)\) mark an alias hit in text \(x\).  Edit success is
\(ES_b=\Pr[h_{\mathrm{new}}(a_b)\land\neg h_{\mathrm{old}}(a_b)]\); we call \(ES_{B_0}\) direct-answer edit success and \(ES_{B_3}\) after-chain edit success.
Among requests successful at \(B_0\), permissive reversion \(RR_b\) records any old-value hit in the later answer, whereas strict lexical displacement \(RR^s_b\) also requires the new value absent.  These are nested operational endpoints with different error profiles, not mathematical bounds on a latent semantic rate.  Chain leakage \(CLR_b\) records an old-value mention in the chain without claiming semantic commitment.  Locality is only a target-value non-leakage check on the neighbor answer, not a general preservation measure.

\subsection{A Signed Think-Span Intervention}
\label{sec:control-design}

Inside the think span we add a fixed logit penalty to the first tokens of the old value's aliases; decoding is otherwise unchanged, and no penalty applies while the answer is written. \section{Discussion \& Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

Our claims are about six fixed checkpoints, not scale: we report every cell, infer no ordered trend, and read the two lineages' agreement as bounding backbone idiosyncrasy rather than recipe-specificity, since both share one teacher and recipe (Section~\ref{sec:gap-results}).  They are also about one filtered request pool, fixed by a single checkpoint and reused at all six (Section~\ref{sec:gap-protocol}).  No judge panel has a human-audited subsample, and where a judge family is recorded it is a single one, so shared judge bias would not surface.

The headline rests on one editor and one dataset: MEMIT enters only through the transfer replication; a second benchmark, edit family, and non-counterfactual edits are untested.

Our endpoints are lexical: edit success, reversion, and displacement match curated aliases with boundary awareness, so a semantically right answer outside the alias set scores as a miss.  Locality is narrower still---a target-value non-leakage rate, where a refusal or fluent non-answer counts as non-leaking rather than preserved behaviour.

Natural-chain mediation remains unidentified: the manifest-pinned replay vehicle failed its own validity gate (Section~\ref{sec:diagnosis-replay}).  Identifying it needs a different experiment---substituting lexically orthogonal chains with the edit held fixed---which is future work we claim nothing from.

Finally, the guard's cost on unedited queries sits inside a \(0.02\) absolute-accuracy gate at the point estimate on full benchmark sets---\(.000\) on \(1319\) GSM8K items \citep{cobbe2021gsm8k} and \(-.010\) on \(500\) MATH items \citep{hendrycks2021math}---while the accompanying 90\% intervals, \([-.022,.022]\) and \([-.061,.041]\), are wider than that gate. \section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

An edit that answers correctly on demand has not been shown to survive the reasoning the model will actually do.  Evaluating the same edit at zero thinking and after a native chain exposes a gap that direct-answer validation cannot see: not matched by the unedited base's own rate on the largest Qwen checkpoint, absent there under a fixed canned thought, unchanged under sampling, and present at the largest checkpoint we tested in each of two lineages.  That failure nonetheless admits a chain-local causal control point.

\section*{Ethical Statement}

CounterFact writes a counterfactual over a real-world value, so our framing inverts: each reversion becomes the model restoring a truth we engineered away, the suppressor a tool for silencing true reasoning.  At frozen weights the readings are symmetric: a corrective and an adversarial update are the same parameter change.  Our endpoints match the value the edit replaced, which in CounterFact is also the true one, so nothing here separates reasserting a prior from tracking truth.  The dual-use reading is real; three properties bound this research diagnostic.  It is edited-query gated, firing only on the subject and relation whose edit is defended (Section~\ref{sec:control-design}); it is no general truth-suppression capability.  It steers at inference time, updating no weights and applying no optimization toward concealment.  And no censused reversion took the associative route that leaves no verbalized trace (\(0\) of \(41\)).  That census is conditioned on reversion and is not a sample of unguarded use, so the zero bounds traceless reversion rather than excluding it, and we do not claim that the absence of such a trace could serve as a monitor. % RJ: [{"claim":"ET-assoc","display":"0","pointer":"/rq2_taxonomy/p0_corrected_taxonomy/dist/Associative","format":"integer"},{"claim":"ET-n41","display":"41","pointer":"/rq2_taxonomy/p0_corrected_taxonomy/n","format":"integer"}]
