% ---- VARIANT A — phenomenon-forward (~152 words) ----
\textbf{Abstract (A).}
On native R1-style \emph{reasoning} models, parametric knowledge edits that succeed at zero thinking can be overturned once a genuine chain unfolds: on R1-Distill-Qwen-32B/ROME/CounterFact, natural reasoning reverts $19.3\%$ of zero-thinking successes (ES $0.595\!\to\!0.495$). A bounded cloze re-probe confirms that the edit remains installed in all $23$ reverted cases, while a neutral three-judge taxonomy over $50$ committed reversions finds predominantly reconstructive routes (Bridge $68\%$, Recall $22\%$, Reflective-override $8\%$, Associative $2\%$; Fleiss' $\kappa{=}0.790$). A pre-registered chain-only intervention then halves reversion ($0.193\!\to\!0.085$), removes the $+10.6$\,pp thinking tax, and beats a same-relation strong-competitor control on every answer metric without harming locality; the answer-level repair replicates at 14B. The unedited 32B base already recalls the old fact at $0.765$. Across two families and six fixed checkpoints, the ES-drop pattern is consistent with a size/capability-associated vulnerability, but remains supporting rather than a scaling claim. Scope: native R1 distills and CounterFact; ROME carries the causal battery, with MEMIT providing a one-scale phenomenon replication.

% ---- VARIANT B — mechanism-and-fix-forward (~150 words) ----
\textbf{Abstract (B).}
We study why test-time reasoning undoes installed knowledge edits. On R1-Distill-Qwen-32B (ROME/CounterFact), a natural chain reverts $19.3\%$ of zero-thinking successes. A $23$-case cloze installation check finds the edited value still wins after reversion, ruling out the simple account that generation erased the update; neutral judges instead classify $68\%$ of $50$ committed reversions as bridge-based reconstruction, with flat recall a real $22\%$ minority. We then intervene only inside \texttt{<think>}: suppressing the safe first-token variants of the old value at $\alpha{=}8$ raises ES@$B_3$ from $0.495$ to $0.631$ and lowers RR from $0.193$ to $0.085$. The effect is signed, dose-controlled, and exceeds a same-relation strong-competitor control; answer-level repair replicates at 14B. The unedited 32B base recalls the old fact at $0.765$ but shows no systematic thinking drift toward it. A six-checkpoint, two-family ES-drop pattern provides supporting evidence of a size/capability association, not a scaling law. Native R1 and CounterFact only; the causal battery is ROME-specific, while MEMIT replicates the phenomenon at 14B.

% ---- VARIANT C — eval-caution + capability (~151 words) ----
\textbf{Abstract (C).}
Knowledge edits are usually validated without allowing a reasoning model to think. Under generative word-boundary scoring---which removes roughly $2\times$ substring/alias inflation and agrees with an independent strict-reversion judge ($\kappa{=}0.836$)---natural reasoning reverts $19.3\%$ of zero-thinking successes on R1-Distill-Qwen-32B (ROME/CounterFact). A bounded $23$-case cloze check shows that the update remains installed after reversion; a separate neutral taxonomy over $50$ committed reversions finds Bridge/Recall/Reflective/Associative routes at $68/22/8/2\%$ ($\kappa{=}0.790$). A pre-registered, signed, dose-controlled intervention that suppresses old-value first-token variants only in the chain halves reversion ($0.193\!\to\!0.085$), removes the thinking tax, and outperforms a same-relation strong-competitor control without harming locality; repair replicates at 14B. The unedited 32B base recalls the old fact at $0.765$. The answer-level ES-drop pattern grows across two families and six fixed checkpoints, but we treat this as supporting size/capability association, not a scaling law. Native R1 and CounterFact only; ROME carries the causal evidence, MEMIT a one-scale phenomenon replication.


% ============================== §1 INTRODUCTION ==============================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Parametric knowledge editing---ROME, MEMIT, AlphaEdit and their descendants---promises to insert, update, or correct a fact directly in a model's weights without retraining. Its success is almost universally measured under a \emph{zero-thinking} protocol: the edited fact is queried directly, and edit success (ES) is read off the immediate next-token prediction or a short greedy continuation. Under this protocol, a single ROME edit on \mbox{R1-Distill-Qwen-32B} succeeds on $59.5\%$ of CounterFact cases.\footnote{Generative-scoring ES with word-boundary matching, $n{=}200$; see \S\ref{sec:setup}. We deliberately separate this from logit-side \texttt{rewrite\_acc}, which is not comparable.} But the models that most need reliable editing are increasingly \emph{reasoning} models that, by default, emit a long chain-of-thought (CoT) before answering. The zero-thinking number says nothing about what the edit is worth once the model is allowed to think.

\paragraph{Thinking reverts an installed edit---and we test the chain-content hypothesis.}
When we let the same edited model reason---moving from $B_0$ (no thinking) to $B_3$ (natural CoT)---ES on 32B falls from $59.5\%$ to $49.5\%$ (paired drop $10.6$pp, 95\% CI $[3.0,18.2]$), the old answer appears in $54.5\%$ of chains, and $19.3\%$ of zero-thinking successes are overturned in the final answer. The central question is not merely whether this happens, which nearby work already foreshadows, but what part of the computation carries the failure and whether that part can be manipulated without writing the answer. Our load-bearing evidence is therefore the answer-level chain intervention in RQ3; the cloze probe is an installation sanity check and the route taxonomy is descriptive. Across two families and six fixed checkpoints, the paired ES drop is negligible at the low end and significant at the matched high end of each family ($10.6$pp at Qwen-32B; $10.7$pp at Llama-70B). We treat that pattern as a supporting size/capability association. Raw CLR also rises, but its scale trend disappears after subtracting each model's unedited chain-leak floor, so CLR is not evidence of edit-specific emergence.

\paragraph{The mechanism: reasoning bypasses an edit that is still intact.}
Why does thinking undo an edit? A simple hypothesis is that long generation somehow erases the update. A bounded logit-lens re-probe provides a useful sanity check against that account: in all $23$ reverted cases, the edited value still wins at the original cloze, and the old-over-new band lies at decoder layers $7$--$11$, immediately upstream of the ROME edit at layer $12$, before the gap turns positive after the edit. This is not a complete circuit-level explanation---ROME is optimized to change precisely this readout, and the probe has no causal control---but it establishes that the observed reversion coexists with an installed edit. The independent evidence about what drives the answer comes from the route census and, most importantly, the chain-only intervention.

\paragraph{A chain-only causal fix.}
The working hypothesis is actionable: if old-value content in the chain contributes to reversion, penalizing its safe first-token variants inside \texttt{<think>}---never in the answer---should change the untouched answer. At $\alpha=8$, RR falls $0.193\!\to\!0.085$, ES@$B_3$ rises $0.495\!\to\!0.631$, the paired thinking tax changes $+0.106\!\to\!-0.035$, and locality stays flat. The effect reverses when $o_\text{new}$ is suppressed and exceeds a same-relation strong-competitor control. A pre-registered within-case kill test further finds the repair only where the baseline chain contains old-value content, but its stratification is observational and floor-limited. We therefore claim a signed causal handle and near-necessity evidence, not an identified unique natural mediator.\footnote{$\Delta$CLR is partly mechanical because the intervention and leakage metric share the think span; RR, RR$_s$, and ES on the untouched answer are the load-bearing endpoints.}

\paragraph{Honest positioning.}
We do not claim to be the first to observe any ingredient in isolation. SCR/ReCoE and ThinkEval show that realistic or multi-step inference exposes failures hidden by teacher forcing; CRANE~\citep{crane2026} reports a reasoning chain rejecting an injected edit under multimodal conflict; Superficial Editing establishes that old knowledge can remain encoded after editing; and DISCO gives a training-free answer-level correction. Our contribution is the controlled conjunction: a native-R1 zero-versus-real-thinking axis, edit-specific and cross-checkpoint answer erosion, a chain-localized signed intervention whose answer effect exceeds a strong same-relation control, and a CoT-aware scoring correction. The novelty is therefore experimental and causal structure, not priority over the underlying phenomenon or non-erasure premise.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A chain-only causal mechanism and fix (headliner).} On R1-Distill-Qwen-32B (ROME/CounterFact), suppressing safe first-token variants of $o_\text{old}$ only within \texttt{<think>}---never the answer---removes the thinking tax (ES drop $0.106\!\to\!-0.035$) and halves reversion (RR $0.193\!\to\!0.085$). The effect is signed, dose-controlled, and exceeds a same-relation strong-competitor control (T$-$C: ES $+0.121$, RR $-0.094$, RR$_s$ $-0.057$; all paired CIs exclude zero), while locality remains flat. Answer-level repair replicates at 14B. The unedited 32B base already recalls $o_\text{old}$ at $0.765$ but has no systematic thinking drift toward it.
  \item \textbf{Dynamic non-erasure and route evidence inside native CoT.} A bounded cloze installation check finds the edit still predicts $o_\text{new}$ in $23/23$ reverted cases; the negative gap lies at decoder layers $7$--$11$, upstream of edit layer $12$, and peaks after the edit near decoder layer $58$. Separately, neutral judges classify $50$ committed reversions as Bridge $68\%$, Recall $22\%$, Reflective-override $8\%$, and Associative $2\%$ ($\kappa{=}0.790$). We use the logit lens as installation sanity, not as a stand-alone causal mechanism.
  \item \textbf{A size/capability-associated erosion pattern (supporting trend).} Across two R1-distilled families and six fixed checkpoints, the paired ES drop grows from negligible low-end effects to significant, near-identical high-end effects (Qwen-32B $0.106$; Llama-70B $0.107$). The per-case ES-drop association survives base-recall control; RR is borderline, and raw CLR is explicitly demoted because its net-of-base slope contains zero. This is a supporting association, not a smooth scaling law or title-level emergence claim.
  \item \textbf{A measurement correction.} Generative scoring with word-boundary matching---validated against a neutral 3-judge panel ($\kappa{=}0.836$ for strict reversion)---corrects a $\sim$$2\times$ substring/alias inflation of CoT-leakage metrics; we adopt and recommend it for generative editing evaluation.
\end{enumerate}

% ============================== §2 RELATED WORK ==============================
\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Our work sits at the intersection of \emph{parametric knowledge editing}, \emph{test-time reasoning}, and \emph{evaluation methodology}. We organize prior art into four threads and, for each, state explicitly what is already established and what remains our contribution. The space is crowded; we therefore make our boundaries precise rather than claim priority.

\paragraph{Editing fails under realistic reasoning (phenomenon).}
A growing body of work shows that locate-then-edit methods (ROME~\citep{meng2022rome}, MEMIT~\citep{meng2023memit}, AlphaEdit~\citep{fang2024alphaedit}), while accurate under teacher-forced cloze probes, degrade once the model is allowed to reason autoregressively or hop across facts. \citet{he2025benchmarking} (SCR/ReCoE) demonstrate that realistic non-teacher-forced generation can overturn edits; \citet{baser2025thinkeval} (ThinkEval) quantify stale-fact leakage through externally constructed thought graphs; and CRANE~\citep{crane2026} shows a multimodal reasoner rejecting an injected edit when its visual evidence conflicts. Multi-hop benchmarks such as MQuAKE~\citep{zhong2023mquake} likewise expose poor propagation. We treat the existence of reasoning-time edit failure as a \emph{premise}, not our novelty claim.
\textit{Our boundary.} The nearest works use a single decoding regime, externally constructed reasoning graphs, or multimodal visual conflict. We instead manipulate a native R1 model's own thinking budget, verify with an unedited-base control that the answer reversal is edit-specific, show that the effect appears as soon as genuine reasoning content unfolds, and test a size/capability association across two distillation families and six fixed checkpoints. The association is carried by paired ES drop, not raw chain leakage: Qwen-32B ($0.106$, CI $[0.030,0.182]$) and Llama-70B ($0.107$, CI $[0.032,0.182]$) are the two significant high-end cells, whereas the other four contain zero. We report this as supporting structure, not a scaling law or a claim that prior work missed the phenomenon itself (Section~\ref{sec:rq1}).

\paragraph{Edits are masked, not erased (mechanism).}
Recent mechanistic work argues that editing is \emph{superficial}: the original knowledge is not overwritten but remains encoded elsewhere in the network. \citet{xie2025superficial} establish this with residual-stream and attention analyses, and newer non-erasure studies such as One Mask~\citep{onemask2026} and Illusion of Erasure~\citep{illusionerasure2026} reinforce the distinction between suppressing a readout and deleting knowledge; KELE~\citep{xu2024kele} conversely argues that the residue should be actively erased.
\textit{Our boundary.} Non-erasure is prior art. Our $23$-case logit-lens result is therefore used narrowly as an \emph{installation check}: after the answer reverts, the edited value still wins at the original cloze, and the old-over-new band sits at decoder layers $7$--$11$, immediately upstream of edit layer $12$. Because ROME explicitly optimizes this cloze readout and the probe lacks a causal control, it cannot by itself establish how the natural chain produced the answer. Our distinct evidence is dynamic and behavioral: a neutral route census over native CoTs and a signed intervention confined to the chain, evaluated on the untouched answer (Section~\ref{sec:rq3}).

\paragraph{Training-free correction of stale answers (fix).}
Decoding-time and activation-level interventions can steer models away from stale or parametric knowledge without retraining. DISCO~\citep{sun2024disco} contrasts edited- and original-model output distributions; Thinking-Intervention~\citep{thinkingintervention2025} and SALT~\citep{salt2025} intervene during reasoning; ITI~\citep{li2023iti}, DoLa~\citep{chuang2024dola}, and ParamMute~\citep{parammute2025} manipulate inference-time representations or parametric readouts.
\textit{Our boundary.} We do not claim that a short logits processor is algorithmically complex. Its contribution is experimental locus and identification: only the reasoning span is perturbed, the answer logits remain untouched, the effect reverses under a sign control, and suppression of a same-relation strong competitor is behaviorally inert while suppression of $o_\text{old}$ repairs the answer. At 32B, T$-$C improves ES by $0.121$ and reduces RR/RR$_s$ by $0.094/0.057$ (paired CIs exclude zero); the answer-level effect also replicates at 14B. This differs from answer-level correction and establishes a specific causal handle on chain content, while leaving natural-generation necessity as a bounded rather than fully identified claim (\S\ref{sec:rq3}).

\paragraph{Leakage metrics and evaluation methodology.}
A parallel methodological line shows that substring/alias-based matching inflates editing and leakage scores: Mirage/QAEdit~\citep{yao2025mirage} and principled-evaluation and edit-locality critiques document spurious matches, though primarily in non-CoT settings.
\textit{Our boundary.} We extend this concern to CoT leakage: a word-boundary scoring criterion (rejecting sub-token and $<$4-character alias matches) is required to avoid roughly doubling apparent leakage in reasoning traces, and we quantify the inflation ($\sim$2$\times$) directly. All numbers in this paper use the de-contaminated word-boundary scorer and are never mixed with EasyEdit's logit-side \texttt{rewrite\_acc}. We further check that this scorer is not a substring artifact with an \emph{independent semantic validation}: a neutral three-judge panel---prompted only to read an answer and decide whether it commits to the \textsc{old} value, the \textsc{new} value, or \textsc{neither}, never told the edit's status---agrees with our strict reversion metric ($\mathrm{RR}_s$: $o_\text{old}$ asserted and $o_\text{new}$ absent) at Cohen's $\kappa{=}0.84$ (agreement $0.93$, precision $0.92$; $n{=}87$ stratified answers). The loose RR---by design an upper bound that also counts ``edit held but old value mentioned in passing''---agrees less ($\kappa{=}0.39$, precision $0.48$), and the judge confirms this over-count is genuine: true reversion lies \emph{between} $\mathrm{RR}_s$ and RR, exactly the bracket we report. (Judges share our model family, a bias caveat; $33$ further answers were dropped to transient API rate limits and, being mostly non-reversions, would only raise agreement.)

\paragraph{Causal premise.}
Our chain-only intervention builds on evidence that reasoning chains causally shape final answers in R1-distilled models~\citep{anon2025fromreasoning} (From Reasoning to Answer), which we take as the causal prerequisite for attributing reversion to in-chain re-derivation.

\paragraph{Summary of distinctions.}
In sum, the phenomenon (SCR/ReCoE, ThinkEval, CRANE), non-erasure (Superficial Editing and successors), training-free correction (DISCO and reasoning-time interventions), and metric-inflation caution (Mirage) all have precedents. Our distinct contribution is their controlled conjunction: a native-R1 thinking-budget contrast, edit-specific answer erosion with supporting cross-checkpoint structure, and a signed chain-only intervention evaluated on an untouched answer against strong controls.


% ============================== §3 SETUP AND METHODS ==============================
\section{Setup and Methods}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Models and Editing Backbone}
We study \emph{native} R1-style reasoning models that emit an explicit chain-of-thought delimited by atomic \verb|<think>|/\verb|</think>| tokens before answering. Our capability axis spans \emph{two} R1-distilled families and six scales: \textsc{R1-Distill-Qwen} (math-specialized base) at 1.5B/7B/14B/32B and \textsc{R1-Distill-Llama} (general-purpose base) at 8B/70B. Both use the DeepSeek R1 chat format. Two measurement details we surface up front. (i) \emph{BOS.} Both tokenizers set \texttt{add\_bos\_token=False}; an on/off A/B on Qwen-1.5B shows the leading BOS token has no material effect on Qwen ($\Delta$ES and $\Delta$CLR within one case at $n{=}40$, CIs heavily overlapping), so our legacy BOS-free Qwen runs are valid and the cross-family comparison is not BOS-confounded. Llama degenerates without BOS and is therefore run with it. (ii) \emph{Cross-architecture transfer.} ROME hyper-parameters are ported from the Qwen config; on Llama this over-edited (locality collapse) until we lowered $\mathrm{clamp\_norm\_factor}$ to $2$ for Llama-8B (selected by a locality scan to correct the transfer-induced over-edit; Llama-70B retains the default $4$). The erosion metrics do not hinge on this choice: across $\mathrm{clamp}\in\{2,3,4\}$ on Llama-8B, CLR stays $0.31$--$0.33$, so the cross-family trend is robust to the clamp value (locality is what changes, reaching $\mathrm{Loc}{=}0.835$ at clamp $2$, marginally below our $0.85$ floor). The Llama-70B run uses model parallelism (fp32) and is reported on $n{=}187$ after dropping 13 cases whose generations were numerically degenerate, flagged by a content-based, length-invariant detector. Because degenerate generations are themselves edit failures, dropping them could bias the estimate; we therefore (a) verify the same detector drops $\sim$0 cases on the stable Qwen/Llama-8B runs and (b) report the 70B point both with and without the filter (\S\ref{sec:limitations}); at this sample size its ES drop ($0.107$, CI $[0.032,0.182]$) is now individually significant, matching Qwen-32B at the high end. The Qwen-7B distill is specialized from a \emph{math} base (Qwen2.5-Math); a base probe confirms the unedited 7B already recalls the target facts at $0.59$, so its behavior reflects a math-specialization confound rather than a knowledge bottleneck (\S\ref{sec:limitations})---a confound the cross-family comparison is designed to neutralize. We treat the resulting six-point, two-family progression as a \emph{capability contrast}, never a smooth scaling law.

Edits are applied with \textbf{ROME} as the primary editor on \textbf{CounterFact}; \textbf{MEMIT} supplies a matched 14B phenomenon replication. The full causal-control battery remains ROME-specific. We deliberately keep parametric editing rather than replacing it with retrieval, which lets us ask whether reasoning erodes an installed weight update (\S\ref{sec:rq2}). Edit layers and locality thresholds are selected on \emph{generative} $B_0$ efficacy, never the editor's logit-level \texttt{rewrite\_acc}; on the 7B/Math base this selects an early-middle layer under a $\mathrm{Loc}\!\ge\!0.85$ target.

\subsection{Single-Edit Protocol}
Each test fact is evaluated under a strict single-edit protocol: (i)~install one edit $(s,r,o_{\mathrm{old}}\!\to\!o_{\mathrm{new}})$; (ii)~generate at \emph{every} thinking budget while the edit is live; (iii)~restore the base weights before the next fact. Editing and restoring one request at a time eliminates cross-edit accumulation and isolates the effect of test-time reasoning on a clean, freshly installed edit.

A subtle but decisive implementation point: with EasyEdit, ROME/MEMIT must be invoked with \texttt{sequential\_edit=True}. Under \texttt{sequential\_edit=False} the library copies the original weights back \emph{before} \texttt{edit()} returns, so all subsequent generation silently runs on the \emph{unedited} base model (we observed byte-identical outputs across nominally different edit layers). With \texttt{sequential\_edit=True} the edit persists through generation and is undone by our own per-fact \texttt{finally:\,restore} hook, preserving the single-edit semantics.

\subsection{Thinking-Budget Axis ($B_0$--$B_4$)}
We expose test-time compute as a five-point budget axis applied at decode time. $B_0$ is \emph{ZeroThink}: an empty, immediately closed thought block (verbatim reproduction of the ZeroThink template of Jiang et al.~2025, as used by R-TOFU). $B_1$/$B_2$/$B_3$ generalize the \emph{LessThink} idea into a token-budget truncation of the natural chain, with caps of $256$/$1024$/$8192$ thinking tokens; for CounterFact-style facts the chain is typically far shorter than $8192$, so $B_3$ acts as an effective ``natural length.'' $B_4$ additionally forces continuation in the spirit of budget-forcing (s1) by suppressing early stops. We generate greedily for the main results; a $0.6\times3$ sampling arm is used for robustness. We report the zero-thinking point ($B_0$) and the natural-length point ($B_3$) as the two anchors of the ``thinking tax.''

\subsection{Generative Scoring (ES / RR / CLR / Loc)}
We score on \emph{generated} text, not teacher-forced logits; this is distinct from the editor's \texttt{rewrite\_acc} and the two are never mixed. For each fact at each budget we measure:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Edit Success (ES)} --- whether the generated answer states the edited value $o_{\mathrm{new}}$.
  \item \textbf{Reversion Rate (RR)} --- the fraction of facts that succeed at $B_0$ but are pushed back to the \emph{old} answer $o_{\mathrm{old}}$ at $B_3$ (an answer-level reversal).
  \item \textbf{Chain Leak Rate (CLR)} --- the fraction of facts whose reasoning chain re-surfaces $o_{\mathrm{old}}$ at least once (a chain-level leak; need not change the final answer).
  \item \textbf{Locality (Loc)} --- preservation of unrelated facts.
\end{itemize}
The \emph{thinking tax} is the paired ES drop $\mathrm{ES}@B_0 - \mathrm{ES}@B_3$ and is the primary answer-level quantity for the cross-checkpoint analysis.

\subsection{Word-Boundary Scoring (Methodological Correction)}
\label{sec:wordbound}
A naive substring match for $o_{\mathrm{old}}$ in generated text, combined with an alias list, systematically inflates leakage. Short ISO/language aliases (e.g.\ \texttt{W}=Vienna, \texttt{in}=India, \texttt{it}=Italian, \texttt{es}=Spain) match inside ordinary words and produce spurious hits. We therefore score with \textbf{word-boundary} matching and drop aliases shorter than four characters. Switching from substring to word-boundary scoring lowers the leakage metrics by roughly $2\times$, which we report as a cautionary finding for generative edit evaluation: substring/alias matching can over-state CoT leakage by about a factor of two (\S\ref{sec:rq1}). All numbers in this paper use the decontaminated word-boundary scorer; pre-decontamination values are not reported.

\subsection{Statistics}
Confidence intervals are $95\%$ percentile bootstraps over \emph{facts} ($10{,}000$ resamples). For temperature-$0.6\times3$ results, all seeds from one \texttt{case\_id} are resampled as a block; treating three generations of the same fact as independent rows is forbidden. Pairwise arm contrasts are case-matched. Nominally we target $200$ edits per checkpoint, but report effective denominators after invalid generations: for Qwen-32B, ES@$B_0$ is the nominal efficacy estimate ($119/200=0.595$), whereas paired ES drop and CLR use the $198$ valid $B_0/B_3$ cases, and RR is conditional on the $119$ zero-thinking successes ($23/119=0.193$). We flag an effect as significant only when its CI excludes zero. The RQ3 N/T/D/P/C predictions and controls are confirmatory; the six checkpoint-specific ES drops and their pooled association are exploratory and are reported as supporting structure rather than a scaling law.


% ============================== §4 RQ1 — ANSWER EROSION ACROSS FIXED MODEL CHECKPOINTS ==============================
\section{Editing Erosion Across Model Checkpoints}
\label{sec:rq1}

\paragraph{Setup recap.}
For each model we apply ROME to a single CounterFact edit, generate under thinking budgets $B_0$ (no chain-of-thought) through $B_3$ with the edit held in place, then restore the base weights (single-edit protocol, \S\ref{sec:setup}). All scores are generation-side and use word-boundary, contamination-free judging (\S\ref{sec:wordbound}; ROME$\times$CounterFact, greedy). We report three quantities: the \emph{ES drop} $\mathrm{ES}@B_0-\mathrm{ES}@B_3$ (the ``thinking tax''), the \emph{chain-leak rate} (CLR, fraction of chains in which the pre-edit answer resurfaces anywhere in the CoT), and the \emph{reversion rate} (RR, fraction of edits that succeed at $B_0$ but are overturned to the old answer in the final answer at $B_3$). \paragraph{Main answer-level pattern.}
The paired ES drop moves from small, sign-uncertain values at the low end to two near-identical, significant high-end effects: Qwen $-0.026/-0.030/0.040/0.106$ from 1.5B to 32B, and Llama $0.015/0.107$ from 8B to 70B. Only Qwen-32B ($[0.030,0.182]$) and Llama-70B ($[0.032,0.182]$) exclude zero. RR also rises descriptively within each family (Qwen $0.061\!\to\!0.193$; Llama $0.144\!\to\!0.175$), but its pooled slope is borderline rather than significant. We therefore call this a \emph{size/capability-associated pattern across six fixed checkpoints}, not a pairwise-resolved progression, smooth scaling law, or capability threshold.

\paragraph{The effect requires reasoning content and survives sampling.}
The $B_0$ anchor is not an empty-template artifact. On 32B, a non-empty canned thought with no generated reasoning produces RR $0.040$, close to ZeroThink's $0.000$, whereas a genuine $256$-token chain raises RR to $0.208$ and yields a paired ES drop of $0.130$ ($[0.060,0.205]$). The effect also survives stochastic decoding: at temperature $0.6$ with three seeds per fact, the ES drop is $0.090$ ($[0.010,0.171]$), close to the matched greedy control's $0.110$ ($[0.035,0.185]$), and RR is $0.264$ with a \emph{case-clustered} CI $[0.195,0.336]$. Thinking content, not an empty wrapper or a single greedy trajectory, is the operative manipulation.

\paragraph{Cross-editor phenomenon replication.}
At Qwen-14B, MEMIT (layers $7$--$9$, $n{=}200$, $\mathrm{Loc}@B_0{=}0.965$) shows the same answer-level erosion: ES falls $0.540\!\to\!0.450$, a paired drop of $0.090$ ($[0.010,0.170]$), and RR@$B_3$ is $0.333$ ($[0.250,0.426]$). This establishes that the phenomenon is not ROME-only. It does \emph{not} generalize the ROME causal repair battery across editors; that is the purpose of X2.

\paragraph{Level versus change: neutralizing the base-knowledge confound.}
Larger unedited models both know and verbalize the old facts more often, which could masquerade as erosion if one confused a static \emph{level} with the thinking-induced \emph{change} to an installed edit. We separate them three ways. First, the unedited 32B base recalls $o_\text{old}$ at $0.765$ under $B_0$, yet thinking changes this to $0.744$: paired $\Delta P(o_\text{old})=-0.020$ ($[-0.080,+0.040]$, $n{=}199$). Thus the edited RR $0.193$ is not inherited from a base tendency to drift toward the old answer. Second, raw CLR does rise with scale, but after subtracting each fact's unedited chain-leak floor the six-checkpoint net-CLR slope is $0.054$ ($[-0.008,0.150]$), so CLR emergence is retired and retained only as description. Third, the per-case ES-drop association is essentially unchanged after controlling for base recall and chain length: slope $0.101$ ($[0.030,0.168]$) before and $0.099$ ($[0.028,0.167]$, $p{=}0.004$, matched $n{=}993$) after control. The association concentrates where the base knows the old fact ($0.127$, $[0.038,0.209]$) and vanishes where it does not ($0.043$, $[-0.158,0.168]$): knowledge supplies the fuel, while the size/capability association concerns the change induced by reasoning.

\paragraph{Statistical scope.}
On the current per-case analysis, the ES-drop slope across the six fixed checkpoints is $0.109$ ($[0.036,0.175]$, $p{=}0.003$, $n{=}1194$); the RR logit slope is $0.644$ ($[-0.113,1.372]$, $p{=}0.052$). These confidence intervals quantify fact-level uncertainty conditional on the selected checkpoints; with only two model families, they do not establish a population law over architectures. The descriptive alignment of Llama-8B with Qwen-7B and Llama-70B with Qwen-32B reduces the obvious Math-specialization explanation, but remains consistency rather than causal identification.

\paragraph{Caveats on the cross-family endpoints.}
Two cross-family points carry measurement caveats that we surface here and re-state in \S\ref{sec:setup}/\S\ref{sec:limitations}. \emph{Llama-8B}: porting Qwen hyper-parameters over-edited Llama, so we set $\mathrm{clamp\_norm\_factor}{=}2$ (selected by a locality scan; Llama-70B keeps the default $4$). This is a per-model locality correction, but it does \emph{not} drive the erosion result: across $\mathrm{clamp}\in\{2,3,4\}$ the CLR stays $0.31$--$0.33$, only locality moves. At clamp $2$, $\mathrm{Loc}{=}0.835$, just \emph{below} our $0.85$ target and reported as marginal. \emph{Llama-70B}: run under model parallelism ($2\times4$, fp32), which produced intermittent numerically-unstable generations; we drop $13$ such cases via a content-based, length-invariant degeneracy detector, yielding $n{=}187$. Because degenerate generations are themselves edit failures, this discard is \emph{not} unbiased; we therefore report the point both with and without the filter. At this sample size the 70B ES drop ($0.107$, CI $[0.032,0.182]$) is now \emph{individually significant}, matching Qwen-32B at the high end---so it is an independently significant result rather than a directional anchor only; the content-triggered discard remains the one caveat on its point estimate.

\paragraph{The 7B confound (within Qwen).}
\textsc{R1-Distill-Qwen-7B} is distilled from Qwen2.5-\emph{Math}-7B, so within the Qwen family the $7\text{B}\!\to\!14\text{B}\!\to\!32$B axis mixes scale with math specialization; the flat-to-negative 7B ES drop is therefore not clean evidence of ``no erosion at small scale.'' Importantly, this is \emph{not} a knowledge bottleneck: a base-knowledge probe shows the unedited 7B already recalls the target facts at $0.59$, so its low post-edit ES reflects edit/reasoning interaction, not ignorance. The cross-family comparison above is precisely what neutralizes this confound at the family level: Llama (general) reproduces Qwen's (math) erosion magnitude at matched scale, so the trend is not an artifact of the Qwen math lineage.

\paragraph{Beyond single-hop cloze: erosion replicates on multi-hop propagation.}
A reviewer might worry the erosion is an artifact of single-hop cloze scoring. We test this with a multi-hop propagation probe on \textsc{R1-Distill-Llama-8B} (MQuAKE-CF): we apply ROME to a single fact, then ask a \emph{two-hop} question whose surface never names $o_\text{old}$, and measure whether the edit propagates to the downstream answer and whether thinking erodes that propagation. To make the test informative we first gate to a clean residual pool ($n{=}150$)---dropping cases the unedited base already answers (prior-dominated) and one degenerate relation template whose new-gold answer collapses onto a single entity. On the cases where the edit \emph{does} propagate at zero thinking (answers $o_\text{new}$'s downstream consequence and not $o_\text{old}$'s; $n_{B_0}{=}48$), thinking erodes that propagation in $18.8\%$ of cases (95\% CI $[0.083, 0.292]$, paired bootstrap), carried by the most diverse, best-powered template (citizenship$\to$head-of-state, $n{=}57$: $0.148$, $[0.037, 0.296]$). This is the \emph{same within-case signature} as single-hop reversion at this scale (8B RR $0.144$, $[0.088, 0.208]$): a real erosion of installed edits masked at the margin. We report the gated within-case estimand rather than the marginal for a structural reason---a two-hop question requires reasoning tokens to traverse the hop, so zero thinking cannot express the propagation at all (marginal $\mathrm{hop\text{-}ES}$ \emph{rises} $0.32\!\to\!0.51$ as thinking both completes un-propagated chains and erodes propagated ones); the net-positive marginal is an artifact of zero-thinking's inability to multi-hop, and the within-case erosion is the matched analogue of single-hop RR. The erosion is predominantly to \emph{neither} answer ($8$ of $9$ eroded cases) rather than to $o_\text{old}$: at multi-hop the edit's propagation \emph{decays to noise} rather than the old downstream fact resurging. We scope this honestly as a \emph{single-template multi-hop pilot}: a single family at one scale, on templated MQuAKE pairs, with pooled significance carried by one relation template (citizenship$\to$head-of-state, P27); the other two templates are under-powered ($n_{B_0}{\le}8$) and individually contain zero. It therefore establishes that the erosion is \emph{not purely a single-hop cloze artifact}, but is \emph{not} a clean or generalized multi-hop scaling result (a multi-relation, multi-scale multi-hop study---including 32B---is future work).

\paragraph{Takeaway.}
Zero-thinking evaluation misses a significant answer-level thinking tax at the high end of both families, and the same phenomenon appears with MEMIT at 14B. The per-case ES-drop association survives base-recall control, while RR remains borderline and raw CLR fails the edit-specific net-of-base test. We therefore carry forward the controlled phenomenon---not a title-level capability law---into the mechanism and repair analyses.


% ============================== §5 RQ2 — MECHANISM ==============================
\section{Why Does Reasoning Undo the Edit? (Mechanism)}
\label{sec:rq2}

RQ1 establishes an answer-level thinking tax at the high end, verifies that it requires genuine reasoning content, and replicates the phenomenon with MEMIT. Here we ask what evidence distinguishes a destroyed edit from a reasoning process that bypasses an edit still present in the weights. The evidence has two deliberately separated roles: a bounded logit-lens \emph{installation check} on 32B, and a neutral route census over $50$ judge-committed reversions from seven cells spanning two families. Neither alone identifies a natural mediator; the causal leverage comes from RQ3.

\paragraph{The edited cloze readout remains installed.}
We re-probe the edited fact at its original cloze after selecting valid reverted cases. In all $23/23$ cases, the final-layer cloze prediction remains $o_{\mathrm{new}}$; the median top-layer new-minus-old gap is $11.51$. This rules out the simplest story in which autoregressive generation physically removes the ROME update before the answer is produced. It is intentionally not treated as a full mechanistic proof: ROME optimizes this very readout, the subset is bounded, and the probe has neither a non-reversion control nor a confidence interval. Its role is installation sanity.

\paragraph{Layer indexing places the sign change at the edit.}
Let $g_\ell=\mathrm{logit}_\ell(o_{\mathrm{new}})-\mathrm{logit}_\ell(o_{\mathrm{old}})$ at the cloze. The stored arrays are hidden-state indices, with \texttt{hs[$k$]} read after decoder layer $k-1$. Thus the negative values recorded at \texttt{hs[8]} ($-0.246$) and \texttt{hs[12]} ($-0.351$) correspond to decoder layers $7$--$11$, strictly \emph{upstream} of the ROME edit at layer $12$---not ``layers 8--12 around the edit.'' The gap becomes positive after the edit and peaks, by the per-case median statistic, near decoder layer $58$. This is consistent with the update installing a late readout over a pre-existing representation, but a logit lens alone cannot tell whether that representation caused any particular natural chain token.

\paragraph{What the installation check does and does not establish.}
The check shows coexistence: an answer can revert while the edited cloze readout remains present. It therefore supports ``bypass rather than erasure'' as the working model, but does not by itself show that a particular middle-layer trace mediated the answer. We next ask what the chains actually do, then test the working model by intervening on chain content while leaving the answer logits untouched.

\paragraph{Neutral taxonomy of committed reversion routes.}
We gate to chains whose $B_0$ answer commits to $o_{\mathrm{new}}$ and whose $B_3$ answer is judged to commit to $o_{\mathrm{old}}$, excluding held edits and non-separable value pairs. Three neutral judges label raw chains with a required quoted span under four routes: \textbf{Recall} (flat assertion of the old relation value), \textbf{Bridge} (derivation through a named intermediate), \textbf{Reflective-override} (the edited value is considered, then explicitly reversed), and \textbf{Associative} (residual/no-quotable-route catch-all). The widened pool contains $50$ committed reversions across seven cells, two model families, and greedy, short-chain, and sampled configurations. Bridge remains modal ($34/50=68\%$), but flat Recall is a real minority ($11/50=22\%$); Reflective-override contributes $4/50=8\%$, and Associative only $1/50=2\%$ (itself a $2$--$1$ split vote). In-population Fleiss' $\kappa$ is $0.790$ ($\bar P=0.893$). All four Reflective-override cases occur in high-capability cells (32B or 70B), an observation too small for a separate emergence claim. As a prompt-robustness companion, the original neutral $17$-case pool yields Bridge $14/17=82.4\%$, Recall $2/17$, Reflective-override $1/17$, Associative $0$, and $\kappa=0.765$. We report the two pools side by side and never combine their percentages.

\paragraph{Relation to ``superficial editing.''}
Superficial Editing and subsequent non-erasure work establish that an edit can alter a readout without deleting the original knowledge. We therefore claim neither priority nor a complete circuit account. Our contribution is to connect a bounded installation check to native-chain behavior and then perturb the chain alone. The taxonomy says what routes are common; the intervention in RQ3 tests whether changing old-value content in that chain changes the untouched answer.

% ============================== §6 RQ3 — CHAIN-LEVEL TRAINING-FREE FIX ==============================
\section{A Chain-Level, Training-Free Fix}
\label{sec:rq3}

Section~\ref{sec:rq2} establishes that reversion can coexist with an installed edit and that committed reversions are usually reconstructive or recall-based rather than residual. This motivates a direct test of the chain-content hypothesis: perturb old-value expression only while the model is thinking, leave the edited weights and answer logits untouched, and ask whether the final answer changes.

\paragraph{Method.}
For each edit, we enumerate $o_\text{old}$ and its safe aliases (word-boundary candidates of at least four characters), form with/without-leading-space and capitalization variants, tokenize each variant, and collect \emph{only its first token id}. At every decoding step inside \texttt{<think>}\ldots\texttt{</think>}, we subtract a constant $\alpha$ from those token logits. This is first-token suppression applied repeatedly, not full-sequence blocking; aliases, alternate tokenizations, and paraphrases can bypass it. The answer span is decoded with unmodified logits. The method is training-free, adds no forward pass, and is gated to edited queries, where $o_\text{old}$ is already part of the edit request. The main operating point is $\alpha{=}8$, $\textsc{scope}{=}\textsc{think}$ on R1-Distill-Qwen-32B/ROME/CounterFact (nominal $n=200$; N/T valid $n=198$).

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~\ref{tab:rq3} contrasts suppression off (N) with target suppression (T). T halves final-answer reversion (RR $0.193\!\to\!0.085$), raises ES@$B_3$ from $0.495$ to $0.631$, turns the paired thinking tax from $+0.106$ to $-0.035$, and leaves locality essentially flat ($0.958\!\to\!0.964$). CLR falls $0.545\!\to\!0.263$, but because the intervention directly acts in the span CLR scores, this reduction is a manipulation check rather than the primary outcome.

\paragraph{Causal interpretation: intervening only on the chain.}
The intervention modifies only thinking-span logits; the post-think answer is decoded normally. Improvement on RR and ES is therefore earned downstream of a chain perturbation rather than mechanically written into the scored answer. This is a causal handle on chain content, but it does not by itself prove that naturally generated old-value mentions are the unique or necessary mediator.

\paragraph{Causal specificity: a pre-registered five-arm battery.}
The arms are \textbf{N} (none), \textbf{T} (suppress $o_\text{old}$), \textbf{D} (suppress $o_\text{new}$; sign control), \textbf{P} (frequency/length-matched irrelevant token), and \textbf{C} (a strong same-relation competing answer). P is behaviorally indistinguishable from N (ES $+0.015$, RR $0.000$, RR$_s$ $-0.009$; all CIs contain zero), while D moves answers in the opposite direction (D$-$N: ES $-0.217$ $[-0.289,-0.144]$, RR $+0.086$ $[0.019,0.152]$). Most importantly, C is inert on answer metrics relative to N (ES $+0.015$ $[-0.046,0.076]$, RR $0.000$ $[-0.056,0.056]$, RR$_s$ $-0.009$ $[-0.047,0.028]$), whereas T beats C on every answer endpoint: ES $+0.121$ $[0.051,0.192]$, RR $-0.094$ $[-0.160,-0.028]$, and RR$_s$ $-0.057$ $[-0.113,-0.009]$. C does modestly reduce CLR relative to N ($-0.071$, CI excludes zero) without repairing the answer, which is itself evidence that a generic change in string-level leakage is insufficient. Together with the signed control and the $\alpha$ sweep, the behavior identifies $o_\text{old}$-specific chain content rather than arbitrary logit perturbation or suppression of any plausible competitor.

\paragraph{A pre-registered within-case kill test survives, but is not causal mediation.}
Stratifying the paired N/T cases by whether the \emph{baseline} chain contains $o_\text{old}$, T reduces RR by $0.289$ ($[-0.444,-0.156]$, $n=106$) in the CLR-positive stratum, but changes RR by only $+0.014$ ($[0.000,0.041]$, $n=90$) when the baseline chain is CLR-negative. Among $118$ $B_0$ successes, answer reversion improves in $43.5\%$ of cases where CLR drops versus $4.2\%$ where it does not. This pre-registered kill test rejects the simple alternative that the repair works equally without baseline old-value chain content. However, the split is observational, the CLR-negative arm has a floor, and ``CLR dropped'' is post-treatment; we therefore call it \emph{near-necessity evidence}, not an identified natural mediator or closed causal loop.

\paragraph{On the partial circularity of $\Delta$CLR.}
We deliberately do not lead with $\Delta$CLR because the intervention and metric share the same span. The load-bearing endpoints are RR, RR$_s$, and ES on the untouched answer. Residual CLR $0.263$ is compatible with alias/paraphrase bypass and with the fact that only first tokens are penalized. We test active paraphrases in X3; no post-hoc sequence-level rescue arm is introduced before submission.

\paragraph{Answer-level repair replicates at 14B.}
Using the same ROME operating point ($\alpha=8$, scope=think), ES@$B_3$ rises $0.515\!\to\!0.650$ (paired $+0.135$, $[0.055,0.215]$), RR falls by $0.101$ ($[-0.192,-0.020]$), RR$_s$ by $0.040$ ($[-0.081,-0.010]$), and CLR by $0.270$ ($[-0.335,-0.205]$). Thus answer-level repair is not a 32B-only point; the within-case near-necessity analysis remains limited to 32B.

\paragraph{Relation to DISCO.}
The closest training-free decoding fix is DISCO~\citep{sun2024disco}, which contrasts edited and original \emph{output} distributions. Our intervention differs in locus: it operates inside the reasoning span and evaluates consequences on an answer span whose logits are untouched. We do not claim stronger remediation or algorithmic complexity; we claim a different chain-localized causal handle, with natural-generation necessity explicitly left only partially identified.

\paragraph{Collateral damage on non-edit traffic.}
The main causal battery is 32B/ROME/CounterFact, with answer-level repair replicated at 14B. By construction the suppressor fires only on edited queries; non-edit traffic is unmodified. We nevertheless test mis-application. The pathological stress test forms the first-token union over the \emph{full CounterFact collection} (about $21.9$k facts), applies it to every span including the answer, and uses it on unrelated GSM8K/MATH queries. This compounds all facts, all spans, and all queries. Under this deliberately unrealistic construction, $\Delta\text{acc}=-0.15$ on GSM8K ($0.535\!\to\!0.385$, $n=200$) and $+0.04$ on MATH ($0.150\!\to\!0.190$, $n=100$); the low absolute baselines are themselves caused by the old truncating harness. Under the deployment-caliber single-fact, think-only, answer-reaching configuration, the full evaluation gives GSM8K $0.868\!\to\!0.868$ ($n=1319$, $\Delta=0.000$, conservative unpaired 90\% CI $[-0.0217,0.0217]$) and MATH-500 $0.618\!\to\!0.608$ ($n=500$, $\Delta=-0.010$, CI $[-0.0607,0.0407]$). The point estimates lie within $\pm0.02$, but the available aggregate-only data do \emph{not} certify equivalence; paired McNemar/TOST language is withheld until per-item off/on outcomes are recorded. The by-construction edited-query gate remains the primary non-edit guarantee.

\paragraph{Dose-response and scope.}
Across $\alpha\in\{0,2,4,8,12,16\}$, CLR falls $0.545\!\to\!0.444\!\to\!0.384\!\to\!0.268\!\to\!0.146\!\to\!0.082$, ES rises to a broad peak near $8$--$12$, and locality remains $\approx0.958$ throughout. Thus $\alpha=8$ is not an isolated selected extremum. A scope=all ablation produces comparable B3 leakage/reversion reduction but raises ES@$B_0$ from $0.580$ to $0.727$ by directly suppressing the scored answer span; it is therefore excluded from the causal interpretation. The reported think-only method never touches that span.


% ============================== §7 LIMITATIONS AND DISCUSSION ==============================
\section{Limitations and Discussion}
\label{sec:limitations}

Our central claim is narrow. Native R1 reasoning can overturn an installed parametric edit; on ROME/CounterFact, a chain-only first-token intervention repairs the untouched answer under signed, dose, placebo, and strong-competitor controls, with answer-level replication at 14B. MEMIT supplies a one-scale phenomenon replication, not a repair replication. The logit lens is an installation sanity check, the route taxonomy is descriptive, and the six-checkpoint size/capability association is supporting rather than title-level. We do not claim a scaling law, novelty of reasoning-time edit failure, novelty of non-erasure, natural-mediator identification, or a deployment-ready universal repair.

\paragraph{A trend across six points and two families, not a scaling law.}
Our axis contains six fixed checkpoints across two distillation lineages. The paired ES drop is significant only at Qwen-32B ($0.106$, $[0.030,0.182]$) and Llama-70B ($0.107$, $[0.032,0.182]$, $n=187$); the other four contain zero. The current per-case ES-drop slope is $0.109$ ($[0.036,0.175]$, $p=0.003$), and remains $0.099$ ($[0.028,0.167]$, $p=0.004$) after controlling for base recall and chain length. RR is borderline ($p=0.052$). Although raw CLR rises within both families, the six-checkpoint net-of-base CLR slope is $0.054$ ($[-0.008,0.150]$), so CLR emergence is explicitly retired. With only two families and selected checkpoints, these intervals quantify fact-level uncertainty conditional on the tested models, not a population law over architectures.

\paragraph{Effect magnitudes are modest.}
The strongest headline model reverts roughly one in five zero-thinking successes ($\mathrm{RR}=0.193$); we neither round this above $0.20$ nor describe it as ubiquitous failure. The repair's relative reduction is large but acts on this modest absolute footprint. We therefore center the contribution on paired answer-level change and controlled intervention; CLR is retained as a chain measurement and manipulation check, not as the capability headliner.

\paragraph{The $\Delta$CLR of our fix is partly circular.}
Our repair acts inside the same span in which CLR is measured, so $\Delta$CLR ($0.545\!\to\!0.263$) is partly mechanical and is not a primary endpoint. The load-bearing outcomes are RR, RR$_s$, and ES on the untouched final answer. Residual CLR can reflect safe-alias and paraphrase bypasses because the method penalizes only first-token variants, not complete semantic sequences. X3 therefore tests active CounterFact paraphrases under the same T/C design; we do not add a post-hoc sequence-level rescue arm before submission.

\paragraph{The 7B endpoint confounds scale with specialization.}
R1-Distill-Qwen-7B is distilled from Qwen2.5-\emph{Math}-7B, a math-specialized base, so \emph{within the Qwen family} the 7B$\to$14B$\to$32B axis mixes a change in scale with a change in domain specialization. The absence of erosion at 7B is therefore not cleanly attributable to low capability alone. We rule out a trivial ``the small model never knew the fact'' explanation: a base-probe check shows the 7B model recalls the targeted facts at $0.59$, so its flat erosion is not a pure knowledge-availability artifact. More importantly, we disentangle scale from math specialization with the \emph{general-purpose} Llama family: Llama-8B reproduces Qwen-7B's erosion magnitude and Llama-70B reproduces Qwen-32B's, so the trend is not an artifact of the Qwen math lineage. (A fully matched general-vs-math pair at identical scale would tighten this further; it is left to future work.)

\paragraph{Two editors for the phenomenon; one editor for the causal repair.}
The six-checkpoint axis and full causal battery use ROME on CounterFact. MEMIT-14B independently replicates the phenomenon (ES drop $0.090$, $[0.010,0.170]$; RR $0.333$), but the repair has not yet been tested with MEMIT; AlphaEdit is absent. Thus the phenomenon spans two locate-then-edit methods, while causal-repair generality remains one-editor. Effective denominators are Qwen-1.5B $196$, Qwen-32B $198$ for paired/chain analyses (with nominal $B_0$ ES retained at $119/200$), and Llama-70B $187$; other main cells use $200$. ROME answer-level repair replicates at 14B and 32B, but the within-case near-necessity analysis remains 32B-only. A single Llama-8B/MQuAKE pilot shows within-case multi-hop erosion, not a broad dataset replication. The full non-edit safety run has aggregate-only off/on accuracies, so we report point estimates and conservative CIs without paired equivalence certification.

\paragraph{Scope: native R1-distilled reasoners.}
Our evidence is restricted to native R1-distilled reasoners that emit an explicit delimited thinking span. We have not established the same budget response on a non-reasoning sibling merely prompted to “think step by step.” Such a comparison would sharpen the regime boundary, but it is not required to distinguish our claim from prior observations: our contribution is the controlled native-thinking contrast, edit-specific base control, and chain-only signed intervention, not the bare claim that some CoT can expose stale knowledge.

\paragraph{The fix uses the old answer, and only by construction.}
The edit request already contains $o_{\text{old}}$, so using it does not introduce a hidden oracle. Operationally, however, the method is narrower than “suppressing the old answer”: it penalizes the first token of $o_{\text{old}}$ and safe aliases (at least four characters) under space/case variants, at every think-step. It neither blocks the complete sequence nor covers arbitrary paraphrases. The method should therefore be read as an edit-aware decoding guard with explicit lexical bypasses, not unsupervised or semantic erasure.

\paragraph{Deployment scope and collateral damage.}
The intervention is gated to edited queries; other traffic is unmodified by construction. Under deliberate mis-application, the pathological first-token union over all $21.9$k CounterFact facts with scope=all yields $\Delta\mathrm{acc}=-0.15$ on GSM8K and $+0.04$ on MATH, with low baselines caused by the truncating harness. Under the single-fact, think-only, answer-reaching configuration, the full runs give GSM8K $0.868\!\to\!0.868$ ($n=1319$, $\Delta=0.000$, conservative 90\% CI $[-0.0217,0.0217]$) and MATH-500 $0.618\!\to\!0.608$ ($n=500$, $\Delta=-0.010$, CI $[-0.0607,0.0407]$). The point estimates lie within $\pm0.02$, but aggregate-only data do not certify equivalence; no McNemar or paired-TOST claim is made.

\paragraph{A by-construction argument with stated preconditions.}
The ``zero collateral damage'' guarantee on non-edit traffic holds under three preconditions that we state explicitly: (a) a single edited fact per query (not a union of many); (b) a routing layer that correctly identifies edited queries, so that false positives do not leak the suppressor onto general traffic; and (c) $o_\text{old}$ is not itself a legitimate intermediate step in the query's own reasoning. Condition (c) marks a genuine blind spot: if an edited query \emph{itself} requires multi-step reasoning and $o_\text{old}$ appears as a correct intermediate, chain-level suppression of $o_\text{old}$ could degrade that query's own reasoning---a regime our non-edit gate cannot observe. CounterFact is single-hop ($o_\text{old}$ is not a reasoning intermediate), so this risk is out of scope for our reported results but limits generality to multi-hop edits; quantifying it is future work \textbf{[pending]}. Crucially, none of this bears on the RQ3 headline results, which are measured on the \emph{answers to edited queries} (RR, ES@$B_3$, Loc); the gate concerns only collateral damage on \emph{non-edit} traffic and leaves the edited-query evidence intact.

\paragraph{Mechanistic claims are scoped to logit-lens.}
The logit lens is scoped to a $23$-case installation sanity check: $23/23$ reverted cases still predict $o_\text{new}$ at the cloze, the negative gap lies at decoder layers $7$--$11$ upstream of edit layer $12$, and the per-case median peak is decoder layer $58$. Because ROME optimizes the probed readout and there is no causal control, this evidence rules out simple update disappearance but does not identify a circuit or natural mediator. The causal evidence is behavioral and chain-localized; non-erasure itself is established prior art.

\paragraph{Positioning relative to prior work.}
Reasoning-time edit failure (SCR/ReCoE, ThinkEval, CRANE), non-erasure (Superficial Editing and successors), and training-free correction (DISCO and related interventions) all have precedent. What this paper adds is a controlled native-R1 zero-versus-real-thinking manipulation, an edit-specific unedited-base control, a supporting cross-checkpoint ES-drop association, and a signed chain-only intervention whose answer effect exceeds both an inert token and a same-relation strong competitor. The repair is mechanistically informative because it perturbs only the chain; it is not claimed as a complex or universal decoding algorithm.
