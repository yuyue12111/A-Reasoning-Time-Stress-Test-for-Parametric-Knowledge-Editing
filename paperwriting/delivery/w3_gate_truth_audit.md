## FALSE — `so the reading does not turn on which control the treated arm is measured against`

**Status.** FIXED: scoped to the two primary endpoints; the strict endpoint does turn on it.

**Evidence.** main.tex:218. For the two primary endpoints the antecedent arithmetic holds: T-P ES .122 vs T-N ES .138 (difference .016) and T-P RR -.095 vs T-N RR -.102 (difference .007), both smaller than the narrower interval width (T-N ES width .112; results.json /rq3/sup_battery/contrasts_B3_mean_ci_p and /cross_arm_paired_ci). But the clause is written unrestricted ("the reading"), and the paper's own Section 5.4 contradicts it for the secondary endpoint: main.tex:236 states the strict endpoint "separates the treated arm from both controls ... but not from the untreated baseline", backed by /rq3/sup_battery/contrasts_B3_mean_ci_p/T_minus_P/RRs = [-0.057,[-0.114,-0.010],0.029] versus /cross_arm_paired_ci/T_minus_N/RRs = [-0.0508,[-0.1017,0.0],0.0732]. So one of the battery's three reported endpoints does flip its separate/does-not-separate verdict with the choice of comparator.

**What a replacement must preserve.** Conclusion protected: the headline effect and the abstract's arm-wise levels are not artifacts of preferring T-N over the pre-specified T-P comparator. True premise that still protects it: "On the primary endpoints the two estimates differ by less than the width of either interval, so the primary reading does not turn on which control the treated arm is measured against (the secondary strict endpoint does; Section 5.4), and the arm-wise levels quoted in the abstract are not an artifact of that choice."
